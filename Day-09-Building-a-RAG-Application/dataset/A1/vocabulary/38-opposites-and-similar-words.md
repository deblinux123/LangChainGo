# Chapter 38: Opposites and Similar Words

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you can:
- Recognize common opposite words.
- Understand simple words with similar meanings.
- Use these words to describe people and things.

## 3. Similar Words

These words have similar meanings, but they are not always interchangeable.

| Word | Similar Word |
|---|---|
| big | large |
| small | little |
| happy | glad |
| beautiful | pretty |
| fast | quick |
| smart | clever |
| begin | start |
| end | finish |
| help | assist |
| speak | talk |
| buy | purchase |
| child | kid |

## 4. Using Opposites in Sentences

1. My house is big, but your house is small.
2. The water is hot, not cold.
3. This question is easy, but that question is difficult.
4. The store is open in the morning and closed at night.
5. My brother is tall, but I am short.
6. The train is fast, but the bus is slow.

## 5. Using Similar Words

1. This is a big house.
2. This is a large house.

Both sentences have almost the same meaning.

Other examples:

- I am happy. / I am glad.
- Please help me. / Please assist me.
- We will begin now. / We will start now.

## 6. Important Notes

- **Old** can describe a person or thing: an old man, an old car.
- **New** is the opposite of old when talking about objects: a new phone.
- **Young** is the opposite of old when talking about age: a young person.
- **Short** can describe height or length: a short person, a short road.
- Similar words may have different levels of formality. For example, "kid" is more informal than "child."

## 7. Short Dialogue

**Nina:** Is your apartment big?

**Mark:** No, it is small.

**Nina:** Is it old?

**Mark:** No, it is new.

**Nina:** Is it near your office?

**Mark:** Yes, it is very close.
