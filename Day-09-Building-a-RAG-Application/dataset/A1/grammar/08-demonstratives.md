# Chapter 08: Demonstratives — This, That, These, Those

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Use this, that, these, and those correctly.
- Identify whether something is near or far.
- Distinguish between singular and plural demonstratives.
- Ask and answer simple questions about objects and people.
- Use demonstratives with nouns and without nouns.

## 2. What Are Demonstratives?

Demonstratives are words we use to identify specific people, animals, or things.

There are four basic demonstratives:

- This
- That
- These
- Those

We choose the correct word based on two things:

1. Is the object near or far?
2. Is it singular or plural?

## 3. This — Singular and Near

Use **this** for one person or thing that is near you.

Structure:

**This + singular noun**

Examples:

- This book is interesting.
- This is my phone.
- This chair is comfortable.
- This student is very friendly.
- This apple is delicious.

## 4. That — Singular and Far

Use **that** for one person or thing that is far from you.

Structure:

**That + singular noun**

Examples:

- That building is very tall.
- That is my car.
- That house is beautiful.
- That man is my teacher.
- That dog is friendly.

## 5. These — Plural and Near

Use **these** for two or more people or things that are near you.

Structure:

**These + plural noun**

Examples:

- These books are new.
- These are my friends.
- These apples are fresh.
- These shoes are comfortable.
- These students are from California.

## 6. Those — Plural and Far

Use **those** for two or more people or things that are far from you.

Structure:

**Those + plural noun**

Examples:

- Those buildings are very old.
- Those are my parents.
- Those cars are expensive.
- Those people are waiting outside.
- Those birds are beautiful.

## 7. Comparison Table

| Distance | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Near | This | These |
| Far | That | Those |

Examples:

- This book is mine. (One book, near)
- That book is yours. (One book, far)
- These books are mine. (Several books, near)
- Those books are yours. (Several books, far)

## 8. Demonstratives Without Nouns

We can use demonstratives without a noun when the object or person is clear.

Examples:

- This is my phone.
- That is your bag.
- These are my keys.
- Those are our friends.

The demonstrative acts as the subject of the sentence.

## 9. Demonstratives with the Verb To Be

Use the correct form of the verb to be.

| Demonstrative | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| This | is | This is a book. |
| That | is | That is my house. |
| These | are | These are my shoes. |
| Those | are | Those are my friends. |

Important:

- This is correct.
- That is correct.
- These are correct.
- Those are correct.

Incorrect:

- This are a book.
- These is my shoes.

## 10. Questions and Short Answers

We can use demonstratives to ask about objects and people.

Examples:

**What is this?**

- This is a pen.
- It's a pen.

**What is that?**

- That is a computer.
- It's a computer.

**What are these?**

- These are keys.
- They're keys.

**What are those?**

- Those are chairs.
- They're chairs.

We can also ask:

- Is this your phone?
- Is that your car?
- Are these your books?
- Are those your friends?

Short answers:

| Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| Is this your bag? | Yes, it is. | No, it isn't. |
| Is that your car? | Yes, it is. | No, it isn't. |
| Are these your keys? | Yes, they are. | No, they aren't. |
| Are those your books? | Yes, they are. | No, they aren't. |

## 11. Demonstratives in Everyday American English

These expressions are common in everyday conversations:

- What's this?
- What's that?
- What are these?
- What are those?
- This one, please.
- That one looks nice.
- These are really good.
- Those are too expensive.

In spoken American English, contractions are common:

- What is this? → What's this?
- What is that? → What's that?
- What are these? → What're these? (Less common in writing; often pronounced naturally in speech.)

## 12. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| This are my books. | These are my books. | Use these with plural nouns. |
| These is my phone. | This is my phone. | Use this with one nearby object. |
| That are my shoes. | Those are my shoes. | Use those with plural distant objects. |
| Those is my car. | That is my car. | Use that with one distant object. |
| This books are new. | These books are new. | This is singular. |
| Those book are old. | Those books are old. | Those requires a plural noun. |

## 15. Chapter Summary

- This refers to one nearby person or thing.
- That refers to one distant person or thing.
- These refers to multiple nearby people or things.
- Those refers to multiple distant people or things.
- Use is with this and that.
- Use are with these and those.
- Demonstratives can appear before nouns or independently.
- Use What is this? and What are those? to ask about objects.
