# Chapter 11: Prepositions of Time — In, On, At

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Use in, on, and at to talk about time.
- Talk about months, years, dates, and days.
- Tell someone when an event happens.
- Use common time expressions in everyday American English.
- Recognize common mistakes with time prepositions.

## 2. What Are Prepositions of Time?

Prepositions of time tell us when something happens.

Examples:

- I wake up at 7:00.
- My birthday is in July.
- We have English class on Monday.
- She was born in 2000.

The three most common prepositions of time are:

- In
- On
- At

## 3. Using IN

Use **in** for longer periods of time, such as:

- Months
- Years
- Seasons
- Centuries
- Parts of the day

### Months

Examples:

- My birthday is in March.
- School starts in September.
- It is very hot in August.

### Years

Examples:

- I was born in 1998.
- She graduated in 2022.
- They moved to the United States in 2020.

### Seasons

Examples:

- It snows in winter.
- Flowers grow in spring.
- We go swimming in summer.
- The leaves change color in fall.

### Parts of the Day

Examples:

- I study in the morning.
- She works in the afternoon.
- We watch TV in the evening.

Important: We normally say **at night**, not "in the night," when talking generally about nighttime.

Example:

- I usually sleep at night.

## 4. Using ON

Use **on** for specific days and dates.

### Days of the Week

Examples:

- I work on Monday.
- We have a meeting on Friday.
- She visits her parents on Sundays.

### Dates

Examples:

- My birthday is on July 15.
- The class starts on September 3.
- The event is on December 25.

### Special Days

Examples:

- We have dinner together on Christmas Day.
- I usually relax on my birthday.
- Many people spend time with family on Thanksgiving Day.

## 5. Using AT

Use **at** for specific times and certain fixed expressions.

### Clock Times

Examples:

- I wake up at 6:30.
- The meeting starts at 9:00.
- The movie begins at 7:45.
- We have lunch at noon.

### Fixed Time Expressions

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| at night | I study at night. |
| at noon | We have lunch at noon. |
| at midnight | The movie ends at midnight. |
| at sunrise | We go running at sunrise. |
| at sunset | The sky looks beautiful at sunset. |

## 6. Comparison: In, On, and At

| Preposition | Used With | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| In | Months | in January |
| In | Years | in 2025 |
| In | Seasons | in summer |
| In | Parts of the day | in the morning |
| On | Days | on Monday |
| On | Dates | on July 15 |
| On | Specific days | on my birthday |
| At | Clock times | at 8:00 |
| At | Fixed expressions | at night |
| At | Specific moments | at noon |

Remember this general pattern:

- **In** = longer periods
- **On** = days and dates
- **At** = exact times

## 7. Common Time Expressions Without Prepositions

Some time expressions do not normally need in, on, or at.

Examples:

- I study every day.
- She works every morning.
- We go shopping next Monday.
- They visited us last week.
- I will call you tomorrow.
- He arrived yesterday.

Incorrect:

- I will call you on tomorrow.
- She works in every morning.
- We met on last Friday.

Correct:

- I will call you tomorrow.
- She works every morning.
- We met last Friday.

## 8. Asking About Time

Use **When...?** to ask about the time of an event.

Examples:

- When is your birthday?
- When does the class start?
- When do you go to work?
- When is the meeting?

Use **What time...?** to ask for an exact clock time.

Examples:

- What time do you wake up?
- What time does the movie start?
- What time is the meeting?

Possible answers:

- My birthday is in June.
- The class starts on Monday.
- I wake up at 7:00.
- The meeting is at 10:30.

## 9. American English Notes

- American English commonly uses **on the weekend**.
- British English commonly uses **at the weekend**.
- Americans usually say **in the fall**, while British English commonly uses **in the autumn**.
- Use **at night** for general nighttime activities.
- American dates are commonly written month/day/year, for example, 07/15/2026.

## 10. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| I wake up in 7:00. | I wake up at 7:00. |
| My birthday is on July. | My birthday is in July. |
| We meet in Monday. | We meet on Monday. |
| She studies at the morning. | She studies in the morning. |
| I work on night. | I work at night. |
| I will visit you on tomorrow. | I will visit you tomorrow. |
| The meeting is in 9:30. | The meeting is at 9:30. |

## 13. Chapter Summary

- Use **in** with months, years, seasons, and parts of the day.
- Use **on** with days and dates.
- Use **at** with exact clock times and expressions such as at night and at noon.
- Do not normally use a preposition before tomorrow, yesterday, every day, or last week.
- Use **When...?** to ask about general event times.
- Use **What time...?** to ask about exact clock times.
- In American English, **on the weekend** is common.
