# Chapter 33 — Object Pronouns and Prepositions

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand object pronouns.
- Recognize the difference between subject and object pronouns.
- Use object pronouns after verbs.
- Use object pronouns after prepositions.
- Use common prepositions with people and things.
- Avoid common pronoun mistakes.

## 2. What Are Object Pronouns?

Object pronouns replace nouns that receive an action or follow a preposition.

They help us avoid repeating names and nouns.

Examples:

- I know Sarah. → I know her.
- She likes Tom. → She likes him.
- We invited John and Mary. → We invited them.
- He called my parents. → He called them.

## 3. Subject Pronouns vs. Object Pronouns

Subject pronouns usually perform the action.

Object pronouns receive the action or follow a preposition.

| Subject Pronoun | Object Pronoun |
|---|---|
| I | Me |
| You | You |
| He | Him |
| She | Her |
| It | It |
| We | Us |
| They | Them |

Examples:

| Subject | Object |
|---|---|
| I like him. | He likes me. |
| She knows us. | We know her. |
| They visit him. | He visits them. |
| We help them. | They help us. |

Remember:

- Use **I** as a subject.
- Use **me** as an object.
- Use **he** as a subject.
- Use **him** as an object.

## 4. Object Pronouns After Verbs

Object pronouns usually come after the verb when they receive the action.

**Structure:**

Subject + Verb + Object Pronoun

Examples:

- I love you.
- She knows him.
- We help her.
- They visit us.
- He calls them.
- My parents like me.

More examples:

- Sarah sees John. → Sarah sees him.
- Tom helps Mary. → Tom helps her.
- I know your parents. → I know them.
- We invited Alex and Sam. → We invited them.

## 5. Object Pronouns After Prepositions

Object pronouns also come after prepositions.

**Structure:**

Preposition + Object Pronoun

Examples:

- with me
- for you
- to him
- about her
- from us
- next to them

Sentences:

- Come with me.
- This gift is for you.
- I am talking to him.
- She is thinking about her.
- They came with us.
- The teacher is standing next to them.

Incorrect: with I

Correct: with me

Incorrect: for she

Correct: for her

## 6. Common Prepositions Used with Object Pronouns

### With

Use **with** to describe being together or doing something together.

Examples:

- Come with me.
- She lives with him.
- They are playing with us.
- I want to talk with you.

### To

Use **to** to describe direction or the person receiving something.

Examples:

- Give it to me.
- I am talking to her.
- Send the message to them.
- He is listening to us.

### For

Use **for** to describe who receives something or who benefits.

Examples:

- This is for you.
- I bought a gift for him.
- She made dinner for us.
- We prepared a surprise for them.

### About

Use **about** to describe the subject of a conversation or thought.

Examples:

- Tell me about him.
- We are talking about you.
- She is thinking about them.
- I know something about her.

### From

Use **from** to describe the source of something.

Examples:

- This letter is from him.
- I received a message from her.
- They got help from us.
- This gift is from them.

### Of

Use **of** in common expressions.

Examples:

- He is a friend of mine.
- She is a friend of his.
- They are friends of ours.
- This is a picture of her.

## 7. Object Pronouns in Questions

We use object pronouns in questions when they receive an action or follow a preposition.

Examples:

- Do you know him?
- Does she like me?
- Can you help us?
- Are you talking to them?
- Is this gift for her?
- Who is waiting for me?

Short answers:

- Do you know Sarah?
  - Yes, I know her.

- Does he call you?
  - Yes, he calls me.

- Can they help us?
  - Yes, they can help us.

## 8. Object Pronouns in Commands

Object pronouns can follow verbs in commands.

Examples:

- Help me, please.
- Call him tomorrow.
- Invite her to the party.
- Listen to us.
- Wait for them.
- Come with me.

Remember that commands usually begin with the base form of the verb.

## 9. Using More Than One Object

Some verbs can have two objects.

Examples:

- Give me the book.
- Send her a message.
- Tell him the story.
- Buy us some food.

We can also use **to** or **for** to introduce the person receiving something.

Examples:

- Give the book to me.
- Send a message to her.
- Buy some food for us.

## 10. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using a subject pronoun as an object

Incorrect: She likes I.

Correct: She likes me.

### Mistake 2: Using an object pronoun as a subject

Incorrect: Him is my friend.

Correct: He is my friend.

### Mistake 3: Using a subject pronoun after a preposition

Incorrect: Come with we.

Correct: Come with us.

### Mistake 4: Confusing he and him

- He calls me.
- I call him.

### Mistake 5: Confusing she and her

- She helps us.
- We help her.

### Mistake 6: Confusing they and them

- They know me.
- I know them.

## 11. American English Notes

Object pronouns are used in the same way in standard American English.

Common conversational expressions include:

- "Come with me."
- "I'll call you."
- "Tell him I said hi."
- "Can you help us?"
- "What's wrong with them?"

In casual American English, you may hear object pronouns after **than** in comparisons:

- She is taller than me.
- He is older than him.

These forms are very common in conversation. More formal writing may use different structures depending on the sentence.

## 14. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- Object pronouns replace nouns that receive an action.
- Object pronouns include me, you, him, her, it, us, and them.
- Use object pronouns after verbs.
- Use object pronouns after prepositions.
- Common prepositions include with, to, for, about, from, and of.
- Subject pronouns and object pronouns have different grammatical roles.
