# Chapter 30 — Adverbs of Manner

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand what adverbs of manner are.
- Explain how someone does an action.
- Form common adverbs by adding **-ly** to adjectives.
- Recognize irregular adverbs.
- Use adverbs of manner in simple sentences.
- Avoid common adjective and adverb mistakes.

## 2. What Are Adverbs of Manner?

Adverbs of manner describe **how an action happens**.

They give us more information about a verb.

Examples:

- She speaks slowly.
- He drives carefully.
- The children play happily.
- My father works hard.
- The dog runs quickly.

In these examples:

| Verb | Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Speaks | Slowly | How she speaks |
| Drives | Carefully | How he drives |
| Play | Happily | How the children play |
| Works | Hard | How my father works |
| Runs | Quickly | How the dog runs |

Ask the question **How?** to identify an adverb of manner.

Example:

- How does she speak?
- She speaks slowly.

## 3. Adjectives vs. Adverbs

Adjectives describe nouns and pronouns.

Adverbs of manner usually describe verbs.

Compare:

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| He is a careful driver. | He drives carefully. |
| She is a slow runner. | She runs slowly. |
| He is a quiet person. | He speaks quietly. |
| She is a good singer. | She sings well. |
| They are happy children. | They play happily. |

More examples:

- She is a beautiful singer. (Adjective)
- She sings beautifully. (Adverb)
- He is a fast runner. (Adjective)
- He runs fast. (Adverb)

## 4. Forming Adverbs with -ly

We often form adverbs of manner by adding **-ly** to an adjective.

**Structure:**

Adjective + ly = Adverb

Examples:

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| Slow | Slowly |
| Quick | Quickly |
| Careful | Carefully |
| Quiet | Quietly |
| Beautiful | Beautifully |
| Clear | Clearly |
| Safe | Safely |
| Polite | Politely |
| Bad | Badly |
| Loud | Loudly |

Sentences:

- Please speak slowly.
- She writes carefully.
- He answered politely.
- The teacher explained the lesson clearly.
- The children laughed loudly.
- Drive safely.

## 5. Spelling Rules

### Rule 1: Most Adjectives

Add **-ly**.

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| Quick | Quickly |
| Slow | Slowly |
| Careful | Carefully |
| Quiet | Quietly |
| Kind | Kindly |

### Rule 2: Adjectives Ending in -y

Change **y** to **i** and add **-ly**.

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| Happy | Happily |
| Easy | Easily |
| Angry | Angrily |
| Busy | Busily |

Examples:

- She smiled happily.
- He answered easily.
- The customer spoke angrily.

### Rule 3: Adjectives Ending in -le

For many adjectives ending in **-le**, change the final **-e** to **-y**.

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| Gentle | Gently |
| Simple | Simply |
| Possible | Possibly |
| Terrible | Terribly |

Examples:

- Please close the door gently.
- Explain the idea simply.
- He behaved terribly.

### Rule 4: Adjectives Ending in -ic

For many adjectives ending in **-ic**, add **-ally**.

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| Basic | Basically |
| Automatic | Automatically |
| Scientific | Scientifically |

Examples:

- The machine works automatically.
- She explained the basic idea clearly.

## 6. Irregular Adverbs

Some adverbs do not follow the usual **-ly** pattern.

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| Good | Well |
| Fast | Fast |
| Hard | Hard |
| Late | Late |
| Early | Early |

Examples:

- She is a good singer.
- She sings well.
- He is a fast runner.
- He runs fast.
- They work hard.
- I arrived early.
- She came late.

Important:

- Good is an adjective.
- Well is usually an adverb when it describes how someone does something.
- Fast can be both an adjective and an adverb.
- Hard can be both an adjective and an adverb.

Incorrect: She sings good.

Correct: She sings well.

Incorrect: He runs fastly.

Correct: He runs fast.

## 7. Where Do Adverbs of Manner Go?

Adverbs of manner usually come after the verb or after the object of the verb.

### Pattern 1: After the Verb

**Subject + Verb + Adverb**

Examples:

- The baby sleeps peacefully.
- She smiled happily.
- He drives carefully.
- The dog runs quickly.

### Pattern 2: After the Object

**Subject + Verb + Object + Adverb**

Examples:

- She speaks English fluently.
- He completed the task carefully.
- They played the game well.
- I read the story quietly.

Incorrect: She speaks fluently English.

Correct: She speaks English fluently.

### Pattern 3: At the Beginning of a Sentence

We can sometimes put an adverb at the beginning for emphasis.

Examples:

- Slowly, he opened the door.
- Carefully, she picked up the glass.
- Quietly, the children entered the room.

This pattern is less common in basic everyday sentences.

## 8. Adverbs of Manner in Questions

We can ask how someone does something using **How**.

**Structure:**

How + do / does + subject + base verb?

Examples:

- How do you drive?
- I drive carefully.
- How does she speak?
- She speaks quietly.
- How does he work?
- He works hard.
- How do they play?
- They play well.

Remember to use the correct auxiliary:

- Do with I, you, we, and they.
- Does with he, she, and it.

## 9. Common Adverbs of Manner

| Adverb | Example |
|---|---|
| Slowly | Please walk slowly. |
| Quickly | He runs quickly. |
| Carefully | Drive carefully. |
| Quietly | The baby is sleeping. Speak quietly. |
| Loudly | They laughed loudly. |
| Happily | She smiled happily. |
| Easily | I can do this easily. |
| Well | He speaks English well. |
| Badly | The team played badly. |
| Politely | Please speak politely. |
| Safely | They arrived safely. |
| Beautifully | She sings beautifully. |

## 10. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using an adjective instead of an adverb

Incorrect: She speaks quiet.

Correct: She speaks quietly.

### Mistake 2: Using good instead of well

Incorrect: He plays soccer good.

Correct: He plays soccer well.

### Mistake 3: Adding -ly to fast

Incorrect: He drives fastly.

Correct: He drives fast.

### Mistake 4: Putting the adverb before the object

Incorrect: She speaks fluently English.

Correct: She speaks English fluently.

### Mistake 5: Confusing hard and hardly

- He works hard. (He works with a lot of effort.)
- He hardly works. (He almost never works or does very little work.)

These two expressions have different meanings.

## 11. American English Notes

These adverbs are common in everyday American English.

Examples:

- "Drive safely!"
- "Speak slowly, please."
- "You did really well!"
- "He works hard."
- "She sings beautifully."

In casual American English, speakers often use **really** or **very** to make an adverb stronger.

Examples:

- She speaks really quickly.
- He drives very carefully.
- They played really well.

## 14. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- Adverbs of manner describe how an action happens.
- Many adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to adjectives.
- Some adjectives have spelling changes before adding **-ly**.
- Some adverbs are irregular, such as **good → well**.
- Adverbs of manner usually come after the verb or the object.
- Use **How** to ask about the manner of an action.
- Some words, such as fast and hard, can be both adjectives and adverbs.
