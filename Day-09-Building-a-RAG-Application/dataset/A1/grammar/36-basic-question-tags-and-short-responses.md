# Chapter 36 — Basic Question Tags and Short Responses

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand basic question tags.
- Use positive and negative question tags.
- Use question tags with the verb "to be."
- Use question tags with the present simple.
- Use question tags with modal verbs.
- Give short answers to yes/no questions.
- Recognize common mistakes.

## 2. What Are Question Tags?

A question tag is a short question at the end of a statement.

We use question tags to:

- Check information.
- Ask someone to agree with us.
- Continue a conversation.

Examples:

- You're a student, aren't you?
- She's your sister, isn't she?
- They live here, don't they?
- He can swim, can't he?

A question tag usually has an auxiliary verb and a pronoun.

## 3. Positive Statements + Negative Tags

When the statement is positive, the question tag is usually negative.

**Structure:**

`Positive statement + negative tag`

Examples:

- You are tired, aren't you?
- She is a teacher, isn't she?
- They are happy, aren't they?
- He can drive, can't he?
- You like coffee, don't you?

More examples:

| Positive Statement | Negative Tag |
|---|---|
| He is happy | isn't he? |
| They are students | aren't they? |
| She can swim | can't she? |
| You like music | don't you? |
| He works here | doesn't he? |

## 4. Negative Statements + Positive Tags

When the statement is negative, the question tag is usually positive.

**Structure:**

`Negative statement + positive tag`

Examples:

- You aren't busy, are you?
- She isn't at home, is she?
- They don't live here, do they?
- He can't drive, can he?
- She doesn't like coffee, does she?

More examples:

| Negative Statement | Positive Tag |
|---|---|
| He isn't tired | is he? |
| They aren't ready | are they? |
| She doesn't work here | does she? |
| You don't like tea | do you? |
| He can't swim | can he? |

## 5. Question Tags with the Verb "To Be"

Use the correct form of **be** in the tag.

| Statement | Question Tag |
|---|---|
| I am late | aren't I? |
| You are happy | aren't you? |
| He is a doctor | isn't he? |
| She is your friend | isn't she? |
| It is cold | isn't it? |
| We are ready | aren't we? |
| They are students | aren't they? |

Examples:

- You're from America, aren't you?
- He's your brother, isn't he?
- They're at school, aren't they?
- It is a nice day, isn't it?

**Special note:**

The common question tag for "I am" is **aren't I?**

Example:

- I'm late, aren't I?

## 6. Question Tags with the Present Simple

When the main sentence uses the present simple without an auxiliary verb, use **do**, **does**, or **don't**, **doesn't** in the tag.

### With I, You, We, and They

Examples:

- You like pizza, don't you?
- They play soccer, don't they?
- We live here, don't we?
- I know him, don't I?

### With He, She, and It

Examples:

- He works here, doesn't he?
- She likes music, doesn't she?
- It looks nice, doesn't it?
- Your brother speaks English, doesn't he?

Negative statements:

- You don't smoke, do you?
- She doesn't drive, does she?
- They don't know us, do they?

## 7. Question Tags with Modal Verbs

Use the same modal verb in the question tag.

Common modal verbs:

- can
- will
- should
- must

Examples:

- You can swim, can't you?
- She can speak English, can't she?
- They will come, won't they?
- He should study, shouldn't he?
- We must leave, mustn't we?

Negative statements:

- He can't drive, can he?
- They won't come, will they?
- You shouldn't do that, should you?

## 8. Short Answers

Short answers are brief responses to yes/no questions.

We usually use:

`Yes/No + subject pronoun + auxiliary verb`

Examples:

- Are you tired?
  - Yes, I am.
  - No, I'm not.

- Is she a teacher?
  - Yes, she is.
  - No, she isn't.

- Do you like coffee?
  - Yes, I do.
  - No, I don't.

- Does he work here?
  - Yes, he does.
  - No, he doesn't.

- Can they swim?
  - Yes, they can.
  - No, they can't.

### With "To Be"

|---|---|---|

| Is he busy? | Yes, he is. | No, he isn't. |
| Are they happy? | Yes, they are. | No, they aren't. |

### With Present Simple

| Question | Positive Answer | Negative Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Do you work here? | Yes, I do. | No, I don't. |
| Does she drive? | Yes, she does. | No, she doesn't. |
| Do they play soccer? | Yes, they do. | No, they don't. |

### With Modal Verbs

| Question | Positive Answer | Negative Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Can you swim? | Yes, I can. | No, I can't. |
| Will he come? | Yes, he will. | No, he won't. |
| Should we go? | Yes, we should. | No, we shouldn't. |

## 10. Question Tags in Everyday Conversations

Question tags make conversations sound natural.

Examples:

**Conversation 1**

A: You're new here, aren't you?

B: Yes, I am.

**Conversation 2**

A: She works at the hospital, doesn't she?

B: Yes, she does.

**Conversation 3**

A: They can't speak Spanish, can they?

B: No, they can't.

**Conversation 4**

A: It's a beautiful day, isn't it?

B: Yes, it is.

## 11. Important Grammar Rules

### Rule 1: Use the opposite form

- Positive statement → Negative tag.
- Negative statement → Positive tag.

### Rule 2: Use the correct auxiliary verb

- You are → aren't you?
- She works → doesn't she?
- They play → don't they?
- He can → can't he?

### Rule 3: Use a pronoun in the tag

Incorrect: Sarah is nice, isn't Sarah?

Correct: Sarah is nice, isn't she?

### Rule 4: Use the correct short answer

Question: Does he work here?

Correct: Yes, he does.

Incorrect: Yes, he is.

### Rule 5: Do not use the main verb again in a short answer

Question: Do you like music?

Correct: Yes, I do.

Incorrect: Yes, I like.

## 12. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using a positive tag after a positive statement

Incorrect: You are happy, are you?

Correct: You are happy, aren't you?

### Mistake 2: Using a negative tag after a negative statement

Incorrect: She isn't busy, isn't she?

Correct: She isn't busy, is she?

### Mistake 3: Using do instead of does

Incorrect: He works here, don't he?

Correct: He works here, doesn't he?

### Mistake 4: Using the wrong pronoun

Incorrect: Sarah is your friend, isn't it?

Correct: Sarah is your friend, isn't she?

### Mistake 5: Giving an incorrect short answer

Question: Can you drive?

Incorrect: Yes, I do.

Correct: Yes, I can.

### Mistake 6: Repeating the main verb

Question: Are you tired?

Incorrect: Yes, I do.

Correct: Yes, I am.

## 13. American English Notes

Question tags are common in American English, especially in casual conversations.

Examples:

- You're coming with us, right?
- It's cold today, isn't it?
- You know him, don't you?
- She's your new teacher, right?

In American English, **right?** is often used instead of a traditional question tag.

Examples:

- You're from Texas, right?
- He works here, right?
- They are your friends, right?

Both traditional question tags and **right?** are natural in American English.

## 16. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- Question tags are short questions at the end of statements.
- Positive statements usually take negative tags.
- Negative statements usually take positive tags.
- Use the correct auxiliary verb in the tag.
- Use pronouns instead of repeating names.
- Short answers use the correct auxiliary verb.
- American English speakers often use **right?** in casual conversation.
