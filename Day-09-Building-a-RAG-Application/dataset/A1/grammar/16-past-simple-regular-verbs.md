# Chapter 16: Past Simple — Regular Verbs

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Talk about completed actions in the past.
- Form the past simple of regular verbs.
- Make positive and negative sentences.
- Ask yes/no and Wh-questions.
- Use common past-time expressions.
- Recognize common spelling and pronunciation patterns.

## 2. What Is the Past Simple?

We use the **past simple** to talk about actions or events that started and finished in the past.

Examples:

- I watched a movie last night.
- She visited her grandmother yesterday.
- They played soccer on Saturday.
- We studied English last week.

The action is finished. It is not happening now.

## 3. How to Form Regular Past Verbs

For most regular verbs, add **-ed** to the base form.

| Base Verb | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| work | worked |
| play | played |
| watch | watched |
| clean | cleaned |
| open | opened |
| visit | visited |
| help | helped |
| cook | cooked |

Examples:

- I worked yesterday.
- She cleaned her room.
- We watched a movie.
- They visited their friends.

## 4. Spelling Rules

### Rule 1: Add -ed

For most verbs, simply add **-ed**.

| Base Verb | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| walk | walked |
| talk | talked |
| listen | listened |
| start | started |

### Rule 2: Verbs Ending in -e

If a verb ends in **-e**, add only **-d**.

| Base Verb | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| live | lived |
| like | liked |
| dance | danced |
| arrive | arrived |

### Rule 3: Consonant + y

If a verb ends in a consonant followed by **y**, change **y** to **i** and add **-ed**.

| Base Verb | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| study | studied |
| try | tried |
| carry | carried |
| cry | cried |

### Rule 4: Vowel + y

If a verb ends in a vowel followed by **y**, keep the **y** and add **-ed**.

| Base Verb | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| play | played |
| enjoy | enjoyed |
| stay | stayed |

### Rule 5: Double the Final Consonant

Some short verbs ending in consonant-vowel-consonant (CVC) double the final consonant before adding **-ed**.

| Base Verb | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| stop | stopped |
| plan | planned |
| drop | dropped |

This rule depends on the word's spelling and stress. Not every verb ending in a consonant-vowel-consonant pattern doubles its final consonant.

Examples:

- open → opened
- visit → visited
- listen → listened

## 5. Pronunciation of -ed

The **-ed** ending has three common pronunciations.

### /t/

After voiceless sounds such as /k/, /p/, /s/, and /ʃ/.

Examples:

- worked
- watched
- helped
- washed

### /d/

After most voiced sounds and vowels.

Examples:

- played
- cleaned
- opened
- lived

### /ɪd/

After verbs ending in /t/ or /d/.

Examples:

- wanted
- needed
- started
- visited

Important: The spelling is always **-ed**, but the pronunciation changes.

## 6. Positive Sentences

Use the past form of the verb for every subject.

### Structure

**Subject + past simple verb + complement**

| Subject | Example |
|---|---|
| I | I worked yesterday. |
| You | You played soccer. |
| He | He watched TV. |
| She | She studied English. |
| It | It stopped suddenly. |
| We | We visited our friends. |
| They | They cleaned the house. |

Unlike the present simple, regular past verbs do not change according to the subject.

Correct:

- I worked.
- She worked.
- They worked.

## 7. Negative Sentences

Use **did not** or **didn't** followed by the base form of the verb.

### Structure

**Subject + did not + base verb + complement**

Examples:

- I did not work yesterday.
- She did not watch the movie.
- They did not visit us.
- We did not play soccer.

### Contractions

**did not = didn't**

Examples:

- I didn't work yesterday.
- He didn't call me.
- We didn't study last night.
- They didn't clean the room.

Important: After **didn't**, use the base form, not the past form.

Correct:
- She didn't watch TV.
- They didn't play soccer.

Incorrect:
- She didn't watched TV.
- They didn't played soccer.

## 8. Yes/No Questions

Use **did** at the beginning of the question.

### Structure

**Did + subject + base verb + complement?**

Examples:

- Did you work yesterday?
- Did she call you?
- Did they watch the movie?
- Did he study English?

### Short Answers

| Question | Positive Answer | Negative Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Did you work? | Yes, I did. | No, I didn't. |
| Did she call? | Yes, she did. | No, she didn't. |
| Did they play? | Yes, they did. | No, they didn't. |

Remember: The main verb stays in its base form after **did**.

## 9. Wh- Questions

Put a question word before **did**.

### Structure

**Question word + did + subject + base verb?**

| Question Word | Example |
|---|---|
| What | What did you watch? |
| Where | Where did she work? |
| When | When did they arrive? |
| Why | Why did he leave? |
| Who | Who did you call? |
| How | How did you travel? |

Examples:

- What did you cook last night?
- Where did they stay?
- When did she finish work?
- Why did you study English?

## 10. Common Past-Time Expressions

We often use these expressions with the past simple:

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| yesterday | I worked yesterday. |
| yesterday morning | She called yesterday morning. |
| last night | We watched a movie last night. |
| last week | They visited us last week. |
| last month | He traveled last month. |
| last year | I studied English last year. |
| two days ago | She arrived two days ago. |
| three weeks ago | We moved three weeks ago. |
| in 2020 | They opened the store in 2020. |

## 11. Past Simple: Regular Verbs vs. Verb To Be

The verb **be** uses was/were. Other regular verbs use **-ed** in positive past sentences.

| Meaning | Verb To Be | Regular Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | I was tired. | I worked yesterday. |
| Negative | I wasn't tired. | I didn't work yesterday. |
| Question | Were you tired? | Did you work yesterday? |

Do not use **did** with was or were.

Correct:
- Was she happy?
- Did she work?

Incorrect:
- Did she be happy?
- Did she worked?

## 12. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| She work yesterday. | She worked yesterday. |
| He studyed English. | He studied English. |
| They stoped the car. | They stopped the car. |
| I didn't worked. | I didn't work. |
| Did you watched TV? | Did you watch TV? |
| She didn't called me. | She didn't call me. |
| Where did you went? | Where did you go? |

## 13. American English Notes

These examples use standard American English.

- **Movie** is commonly used instead of **film** in everyday American English.
- **Soccer** refers to association football.
- **Vacation** is common in American English; British English often uses **holiday** for a trip away from home.
- The regular past-tense spelling rules are generally the same in American and British English, although some individual verbs have regional spelling differences.

Examples:

- We watched a movie last night.
- She visited her parents on vacation.
- I called my friend yesterday.
- They traveled to California last summer.

## 16. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- Regular past verbs usually end in **-ed**.
- Some verbs need spelling changes before adding **-ed**.
- The **-ed** ending has three pronunciations: /t/, /d/, and /ɪd/.
- Use **didn't + base verb** for negative sentences.
- Use **Did + subject + base verb** for yes/no questions.
- Use a question word before **did** for Wh-questions.
- Use past-time expressions to describe when an action happened.

### Key Structures

| Sentence Type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + past verb |
| Negative | Subject + didn't + base verb |
| Yes/No Question | Did + subject + base verb? |
| Wh-Question | Question word + did + subject + base verb? |
