# Chapter 29 — Basic Superlative Adjectives

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand what superlative adjectives are.
- Compare one person or thing with a whole group.
- Form superlatives with **-est**.
- Use **the most** with longer adjectives.
- Recognize irregular superlative forms.
- Avoid common mistakes when using superlatives.

## 2. What Are Superlative Adjectives?

We use superlative adjectives to describe one person or thing that has the highest or lowest degree of a quality in a group.

Examples:

- Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world.
- Sarah is the tallest student in her class.
- This is the most interesting book in the library.
- February is the shortest month of the year.
- That is the most expensive restaurant in town.

A comparative adjective compares two people or things.

A superlative adjective identifies one person or thing as having the highest or lowest degree of a quality in a group.

| Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|
| Taller | The tallest |
| Smaller | The smallest |
| Faster | The fastest |
| More interesting | The most interesting |
| Better | The best |

## 3. Superlatives with Short Adjectives

For many short adjectives, add **-est**.

**Structure:**

Subject + be + the + adjective-est

Examples:

- He is the tallest student.
- This is the smallest room.
- She is the fastest runner.
- That is the cheapest phone.
- Today is the coldest day.

### Common Short Adjectives

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| Tall | Taller | The tallest |
| Small | Smaller | The smallest |
| Fast | Faster | The fastest |
| Slow | Slower | The slowest |
| Long | Longer | The longest |
| Short | Shorter | The shortest |
| Old | Older | The oldest |
| Young | Younger | The youngest |
| Cheap | Cheaper | The cheapest |
| Cold | Colder | The coldest |

## 4. Spelling Rules for Superlatives

### Rule 1: Most Short Adjectives

Add **-est**.

| Adjective | Superlative |
|---|---|
| Tall | Tallest |
| Fast | Fastest |
| Small | Smallest |
| Cold | Coldest |

### Rule 2: Adjectives Ending in -e

Add **-st**.

| Adjective | Superlative |
|---|---|
| Nice | Nicest |
| Large | Largest |
| Wide | Widest |
| Safe | Safest |

### Rule 3: Consonant + Vowel + Consonant

For many short adjectives with this pattern, double the final consonant and add **-est**.

| Adjective | Superlative |
|---|---|
| Big | Biggest |
| Hot | Hottest |
| Thin | Thinnest |
| Fat | Fattest |

### Rule 4: Adjectives Ending in Consonant + y

Change **y** to **i** and add **-est**.

| Adjective | Superlative |
|---|---|
| Happy | Happiest |
| Easy | Easiest |
| Busy | Busiest |
| Heavy | Heaviest |

## 5. Superlatives with Long Adjectives

For many longer adjectives, use **the most** before the adjective.

**Structure:**

The most + adjective

Examples:

- She is the most beautiful girl in the class.
- This is the most expensive hotel in the city.
- That was the most interesting movie.
- He is the most popular singer in the group.
- This is the most comfortable chair in the room.

### Common Long Adjectives

| Adjective | Superlative |
|---|---|
| Beautiful | The most beautiful |
| Expensive | The most expensive |
| Interesting | The most interesting |
| Comfortable | The most comfortable |
| Difficult | The most difficult |
| Important | The most important |
| Popular | The most popular |

Do not normally add **-est** to these adjectives.

Incorrect: the beautifulest

Correct: the most beautiful

Incorrect: the expensivest

Correct: the most expensive

## 6. Irregular Superlative Adjectives

Some adjectives have special superlative forms.

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| Good | Better | The best |
| Bad | Worse | The worst |
| Far | Farther / Further | The farthest / furthest |
| Little | Less | The least |
| Much / Many | More | The most |

Examples:

- She is the best student in the class.
- This is the worst movie I have ever seen.
- That is the farthest city from here.
- He has the least free time in our family.
- This store has the most books.

At A1 level, focus especially on:

- Good → The best
- Bad → The worst

## 7. Using "In" and "Of" with Superlatives

We often use **in** or **of** to identify the group being compared.

### Use "In"

Use **in** before a place, organization, or group.

Examples:

- She is the tallest student in the class.
- This is the biggest city in the country.
- He is the fastest player in the team.
- That is the most expensive hotel in town.

### Use "Of"

Use **of** before a group or a period of time.

Examples:

- She is the youngest of the three sisters.
- This is the best of all the options.
- Today is the coldest day of the year.
- He is the strongest of the two brothers.

## 8. Using Superlatives with Nouns

Superlatives can describe singular or plural nouns, depending on the sentence.

Examples:

- This is the biggest house on the street.
- She is the best student in the class.
- These are the most expensive shoes in the store.
- Those are the smallest boxes in the room.

The superlative form itself does not change for plural nouns.

## 9. Superlatives Without a Noun

We can use a superlative without repeating the noun when the meaning is clear.

Examples:

- Which city is the biggest?
- This restaurant is good, but that one is the best.
- There are many phones here. Which one is the cheapest?
- I like all these books, but this one is the most interesting.

## 10. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Forgetting "the"

Incorrect: She is tallest student.

Correct: She is the tallest student.

### Mistake 2: Using "more" and "-est" together

Incorrect: the most tallest

Correct: the tallest

### Mistake 3: Using "-est" with long adjectives

Incorrect: the beautifulest

Correct: the most beautiful

### Mistake 4: Forgetting spelling changes

Incorrect: the happyest

Correct: the happiest

### Mistake 5: Using the wrong irregular form

Incorrect: This is the goodest restaurant.

Correct: This is the best restaurant.

### Mistake 6: Confusing comparative and superlative forms

Comparative:

- My brother is taller than me.

Superlative:

- My brother is the tallest person in our family.

## 11. American English Notes

These superlative forms are standard in American English.

Examples:

- This is the best pizza in town.
- She is the smartest student in the class.
- That's the most popular restaurant downtown.
- This is the cheapest option.

In everyday American English, speakers often use expressions such as:

- "the very best"
- "the absolute best"
- "the biggest one"
- "the most important thing"

These expressions make the superlative stronger or more conversational.

## 14. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- Superlatives describe the highest or lowest degree of a quality in a group.
- Add **-est** to many short adjectives.
- Use **the most** with many longer adjectives.
- Some adjectives have irregular superlative forms.
- Use **the** before most superlative adjectives.
- Use **in** for places and groups, and **of** for sets or periods of time.
- Do not combine **more** with **-est**.
