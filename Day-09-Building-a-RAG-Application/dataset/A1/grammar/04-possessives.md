# Chapter 4: Possessive Adjectives and Possessive Pronouns

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, students will be able to:

- Express ownership and possession.
- Use possessive adjectives correctly.
- Use possessive pronouns correctly.
- Understand the difference between my and mine.
- Use apostrophe-s to show possession.
- Avoid common possessive mistakes.

## 2. What Is Possession?

Possession means that something belongs to someone.

Examples:

- This is my phone.
- That is her car.
- The house is theirs.
- This book belongs to John.

English uses possessive adjectives, possessive pronouns, and possessive nouns to express ownership.

## 3. Possessive Adjectives

Possessive adjectives show who owns something.

They always come before a noun.

### Possessive Adjective Table

| Subject Pronoun | Possessive Adjective | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | my | This is my book. |
| You | your | Is this your bag? |
| He | his | His car is new. |
| She | her | Her phone is expensive. |
| It | its | The dog is eating its food. |
| We | our | Our house is big. |
| They | their | Their children are happy. |

### Examples

- My name is Alex.
- Your English is good.
- His brother is a doctor.
- Her parents live in Texas.
- The cat is drinking its milk.
- Our teacher is friendly.
- Their house is beautiful.

### Important Rule

A possessive adjective must be followed by a noun.

Correct:
- This is my phone.
- That is her car.

Incorrect:
- This is my.
- That is her.

## 4. Possessive Pronouns

Possessive pronouns show ownership without repeating the noun.

### Possessive Pronoun Table

| Subject Pronoun | Possessive Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | mine | This book is mine. |
| You | yours | The bag is yours. |
| He | his | The car is his. |
| She | hers | The phone is hers. |
| It | — | No commonly used independent possessive pronoun |
| We | ours | The house is ours. |
| They | theirs | The seats are theirs. |

### Examples

- This laptop is mine.
- Is this jacket yours?
- That bicycle is his.
- The red car is hers.
- This classroom is ours.
- Those books are theirs.

### Important Rule

A possessive pronoun replaces the noun phrase. Do not put a noun immediately after it.

Correct:
- This is mine.
- The house is theirs.

Incorrect:
- This is mine book.
- The house is theirs house.

## 5. Possessive Adjectives vs. Possessive Pronouns

The main difference is that possessive adjectives come before nouns, while possessive pronouns stand alone.

| Possessive Adjective | Possessive Pronoun |
|---|---|
| my book | mine |
| your phone | yours |
| his car | his |
| her bag | hers |
| our house | ours |
| their dog | theirs |

### Compare the Examples

- This is my book.
- This book is mine.

- That is her phone.
- That phone is hers.

- This is our house.
- This house is ours.

- Those are their seats.
- Those seats are theirs.

## 6. Possessive Nouns: Apostrophe-S

We can also use an apostrophe and s ('s) to show ownership.

### Singular Nouns

Add 's to a singular noun.

Examples:

- John's car
- Sarah's phone
- My brother's room
- The teacher's desk

Sentences:

- This is John's laptop.
- Sarah's house is beautiful.
- My brother's room is clean.

### Plural Nouns Ending in S

Add only an apostrophe (').

Examples:

- The students' books
- My parents' house
- The teachers' office

### Irregular Plural Nouns

Add 's to irregular plural nouns that do not end in s.

Examples:

- Children's toys
- Men's clothes
- Women's shoes

### Joint Ownership

When two people share one thing, put 's after the second name.

Example:
- John and Mary's house.

When each person owns a separate thing, use 's after both names.

Example:
- John's and Mary's cars.

## 7. Its vs. It's

These two forms have different meanings.

### Its

"Its" is a possessive adjective.

Examples:

- The dog is eating its food.
- The company changed its name.

### It's

"It's" is a contraction of "it is" or "it has."

Examples:

- It's a beautiful day. (It is)
- It's been a long day. (It has)

Important:

Never use "it's" to show possession.

Correct:
- The cat is drinking its milk.

Incorrect:
- The cat is drinking it's milk.

## 8. Possessives in Everyday American English

Possessives are very common in everyday conversations.

### Conversation 1: At School

Alex: Is this your notebook?

Emma: No, it's hers.

Alex: Is that your notebook?

Emma: Yes, it's mine.

### Conversation 2: At Home

Mike: Whose phone is this?

Sarah: It's my brother's phone.

Mike: Is that his?

Sarah: Yes, it's his.

### Conversation 3: Meeting a Friend

John: Is this your parents' house?

Alex: Yes, it's ours.

John: It's beautiful!

## 9. Common Mistakes

Incorrect: This is mine book.

Correct: This is my book.

Incorrect: This phone is her.

Correct: This phone is hers.

Incorrect: That is theirs car.

Correct: That is their car.

Incorrect: The dog is eating it's food.

Correct: The dog is eating its food.

Incorrect: This is John car.

Correct: This is John's car.

Incorrect: The childrens' toys are here.

Correct: The children's toys are here.

## 10. American English Notes

The basic possessive rules are the same in American and British English.

American English commonly uses:

- My apartment
- My mom's car
- My friend's phone
- Our vacation
- Their neighborhood

Possessive forms do not change based on the dialect.

## 13. Chapter Summary

Remember:

- Possessive adjectives come before nouns.
- Possessive pronouns replace nouns.
- Add 's to singular names to show ownership.
- Add an apostrophe after regular plural nouns ending in s.
- Use 's with irregular plural nouns.
- Its shows possession.
- It's means it is or it has.

Examples:

- This is my book.
- This book is mine.
- That is Sarah's phone.
- The dog is eating its food.
