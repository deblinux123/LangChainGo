# Chapter 27 — Conjunctions: And, But, Or, Because

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand what conjunctions are.
- Connect words, phrases, and simple sentences.
- Use **and** to add information.
- Use **but** to show contrast.
- Use **or** to give choices.
- Use **because** to give reasons.
- Build longer and more natural sentences.

## 2. What Are Conjunctions?

A conjunction is a word that connects words, phrases, or clauses.

Conjunctions help us combine ideas and make our sentences more natural.

Compare:

- I like coffee. I like tea.
- I like coffee and tea.

The second sentence connects two ideas using **and**.

In this chapter, we will learn four common conjunctions:

| Conjunction | Function |
|---|---|
| And | Adds information |
| But | Shows contrast |
| Or | Gives a choice |
| Because | Gives a reason |

## 3. And: Adding Information

Use **and** to connect similar ideas or add information.

Examples:

- I like coffee and tea.
- She is smart and friendly.
- We study English and Spanish.
- He works in an office and lives in Chicago.
- My brother plays soccer and basketball.

### Connecting Sentences

We can also use and to connect two complete ideas.

Examples:

- I am tired, and I want to sleep.
- She likes music, and she plays the piano.
- He works during the day, and he studies at night.
- We went to the store, and we bought some food.

### More Examples

- My mother and father are teachers.
- I have a laptop and a phone.
- They speak English and French.
- She gets up early and exercises every morning.

## 4. But: Showing Contrast

Use **but** to connect two ideas that are different or surprising.

Examples:

- I like coffee, but I don't like tea.
- She is tired, but she is happy.
- He is young, but he is very responsible.
- The restaurant is small, but it is beautiful.
- I want to go out, but I have to work.

### Connecting Sentences

Examples:

- I studied hard, but I didn't pass the exam.
- She likes sports, but she doesn't play soccer.
- It is cold outside, but the sun is shining.
- He is busy, but he can help us.

The word **but** shows that the second idea is different from what we might expect.

## 5. Or: Giving Choices

Use **or** to give two or more choices.

Examples:

- Do you want coffee or tea?
- Would you like water or juice?
- We can watch a movie or play a game.
- You can call me or send me a message.
- Is your car red or blue?

### In Questions

Examples:

- Do you want pizza or pasta?
- Are you studying English or Spanish?
- Do you work in the morning or in the afternoon?
- Would you like coffee or tea?

### In Statements

Examples:

- You can stay here or go home.
- We can travel by bus or train.
- I usually eat eggs or cereal for breakfast.

## 6. Because: Giving Reasons

Use **because** to explain why something happens or why someone does something.

**Structure:**

Main idea + because + reason

Examples:

- I am happy because it is my birthday.
- She is tired because she worked all day.
- He studies English because he wants a better job.
- We stayed home because it was raining.
- I drink water because it is healthy.

### More Examples

- I like this restaurant because the food is delicious.
- She goes to bed early because she gets up at 6:00.
- They are excited because they have a vacation next week.
- He is studying because he has an exam tomorrow.

### Because at the Beginning of a Sentence

We can also put the reason first.

**Structure:**

Because + reason, + main idea

Examples:

- Because it was raining, we stayed home.
- Because she was tired, she went to bed early.
- Because I was hungry, I made a sandwich.

When the because-clause comes first, we usually use a comma after it.

## 7. Comparing the Four Conjunctions

| Conjunction | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| And | Addition | I like apples and oranges. |
| But | Contrast | I like apples, but I don't like bananas. |
| Or | Choice | Do you want apples or oranges? |
| Because | Reason | I eat apples because they are healthy. |

### Compare These Sentences

**And:**

- I work in an office, and I study English.

**But:**

- I work in an office, but I don't like my job.

**Or:**

- Do you work in an office or at home?

**Because:**

- I study English because I want to travel.

## 8. Common Sentence Patterns

Learn these useful patterns.

### Pattern 1: Subject + Verb + And + Verb

- I wake up and take a shower.
- She cooks and cleans.
- They study and work.

### Pattern 2: Sentence + But + Sentence

- I am tired, but I am not sleepy.
- He likes sports, but he doesn't like soccer.

### Pattern 3: Question + Or + Choice

- Do you want tea or coffee?
- Is she at home or at work?

### Pattern 4: Main Idea + Because + Reason

- I am learning English because I want to communicate with more people.
- She is happy because she passed her exam.

## 9. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using "because" and "so" together unnecessarily

Incorrect: Because I was tired, so I went to bed.

Correct: Because I was tired, I went to bed.

Also correct:

I was tired, so I went to bed.

### Mistake 2: Using "but" when adding similar information

Incorrect: I like coffee but tea. (when you mean you like both)

Correct: I like coffee and tea.

### Mistake 3: Forgetting the comma in a sentence with two independent clauses

Less clear: I was tired but I finished my work.

Better: I was tired, but I finished my work.

A comma is generally used before and, but, or, when they connect two complete independent clauses.

### Mistake 4: Using "because" without a reason

Incorrect: I stayed home because.

Correct: I stayed home because I was sick.

### Mistake 5: Confusing "or" and "and"

- Do you want tea and coffee? (Possibly both.)
- Do you want tea or coffee? (Choose one.)

## 10. American English Notes

These four conjunctions are very common in everyday American English.

Examples:

- I like pizza and burgers.
- It's cold, but I'm going outside.
- Do you want to drive or take the bus?
- I'm staying home because I'm tired.

In informal American conversation, people sometimes begin a sentence with **And**, **But**, or **Because** to connect it naturally to what was said before.

Examples:

- I wanted to go. But I was too tired.
- And then we went home.
- Because I had work the next day.

In formal writing, it is often better to connect these ideas into complete sentences.

## 13. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- **And** adds information or connects similar ideas.
- **But** shows contrast between ideas.
- **Or** gives choices or alternatives.
- **Because** explains the reason for something.
- Conjunctions help connect words, phrases, and complete clauses.
- Use a comma before and, but, or when they connect two independent clauses.
- When a because-clause comes first, use a comma after it.
