# Chapter 26 — Prepositions of Movement

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand common prepositions of movement.
- Describe how people and objects move from one place to another.
- Use prepositions such as to, from, into, out of, across, through, along, and past.
- Give simple directions.
- Describe everyday journeys and activities.

## 2. What Are Prepositions of Movement?

Prepositions of movement describe the direction or path of movement.

They tell us where someone or something is going, where it is coming from, or how it moves.

Examples:

- She walks to school.
- He comes from work.
- The cat jumps onto the table.
- We walk through the park.
- They run across the street.

## 3. To

Use **to** to describe movement toward a destination.

Examples:

- I go to school every day.
- She walks to work.
- We are going to the supermarket.
- He drives to the airport.
- They traveled to New York.

**Structure:**

Movement verb + to + destination

More examples:

- Come to my house.
- Walk to the station.
- Drive to the office.
- Go to the library.

Important: We usually do not use **to** before the words home, here, and there.

- I am going home. (Correct)
- I am going to home. (Incorrect)
- Come here. (Correct)
- Go there. (Correct)

## 4. From

Use **from** to describe the starting point or origin of movement.

Examples:

- I come from work.
- She walks from home to school.
- This train goes from Boston to New York.
- He is coming from the airport.
- We traveled from Germany to France.

We often use **from ... to ...** to describe movement between two places.

Examples:

- I walk from my house to the bus stop.
- She drives from home to work.
- The bus goes from the airport to the city center.

## 5. Into

Use **into** to describe movement from outside to the inside of something.

Examples:

- She walks into the room.
- The cat jumps into the box.
- He gets into the car.
- They run into the house.
- I put the books into my bag.

Compare:

- The cat is in the box. (Location; no movement.)
- The cat jumps into the box. (Movement from outside to inside.)

## 6. Out Of

Use **out of** to describe movement from inside to outside.

Examples:

- She walks out of the room.
- He gets out of the car.
- The dog runs out of the house.
- I take my phone out of my bag.
- They come out of the building.

Compare:

- He is in the car. (Location.)
- He gets out of the car. (Movement.)

## 7. Across

Use **across** to describe movement from one side to the other side of a surface or area.

Examples:

- We walk across the street.
- She swims across the river.
- He runs across the field.
- They walk across the bridge.
- The children are running across the playground.

Think of crossing something from one side to the other.

## 8. Through

Use **through** to describe movement from one side to another through an enclosed space, passage, or area.

Examples:

- We walk through the tunnel.
- She runs through the forest.
- The train goes through the tunnel.
- He walks through the door.
- They drive through the city.

Compare:

- The children walk across the park. (They cross the area.)
- The children walk through the park. (They move inside the park.)

## 9. Along

Use **along** to describe movement following a line, road, path, river, or other long area.

Examples:

- We walk along the beach.
- She runs along the river.
- They drive along this road.
- He walks along the street.
- I ride my bike along the path.

The movement follows the length of something.

## 10. Past

Use **past** to describe movement beyond or beside something.

Examples:

- Walk past the bank.
- She runs past the school.
- We drive past the supermarket.
- He walks past my house.
- The bus goes past the hospital.

Example in directions:

- Go straight ahead.
- Walk past the bank.
- Turn left at the next street.

## 11. Up and Down

Use **up** and **down** to describe movement in a higher or lower direction.

### Up

Examples:

- She walks up the stairs.
- He climbs up the mountain.
- The children run up the hill.
- I go up the elevator.

### Down

Examples:

- He walks down the stairs.
- They run down the hill.
- She goes down the elevator.
- The ball rolls down the street.

## 12. Onto and Off

Use **onto** to describe movement to a surface.

Examples:

- The cat jumps onto the table.
- He climbs onto the chair.
- She puts the bag onto the desk.

Use **off** to describe movement away from a surface.

Examples:

- The cat jumps off the table.
- He gets off the bus.
- She takes the book off the shelf.

Compare:

- The book is on the table. (Location.)
- She puts the book onto the table. (Movement.)

## 13. Common Prepositions of Movement: Quick Comparison

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| to | Toward a destination | Go to school. |
| from | Starting point | Come from work. |
| into | Outside to inside | Walk into the room. |
| out of | Inside to outside | Get out of the car. |
| across | From one side to another | Walk across the street. |
| through | Inside a passage or area | Go through the tunnel. |
| along | Following a path or line | Walk along the river. |
| past | Beyond something | Walk past the bank. |
| up | Toward a higher point | Go up the stairs. |
| down | Toward a lower point | Walk down the hill. |
| onto | Movement to a surface | Jump onto the bed. |
| off | Movement away from a surface | Get off the bus. |

## 14. Giving Simple Directions

We use prepositions of movement when giving directions.

Example:

**Question:** How do I get to the library?

**Answer:**

- Walk along this street.
- Go past the bank.
- Walk across the bridge.
- Turn right at the traffic light.
- The library is on your left.

Another example:

**Question:** How do I get to the train station?

**Answer:**

- Go down this street.
- Walk past the supermarket.
- Go through the underpass.
- The station is on your right.

## 15. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using "to" before home

Incorrect: I go to home.

Correct: I go home.

### Mistake 2: Confusing in and into

Incorrect: She walks in the room. (when describing movement from outside to inside)

Correct: She walks into the room.

### Mistake 3: Confusing out and out of

Incorrect: He gets out the car.

Correct: He gets out of the car.

### Mistake 4: Confusing across and through

Incorrect: We walk through the street. (when simply describing crossing from one side to the other)

Correct: We walk across the street.

### Mistake 5: Confusing onto and on

Incorrect: The cat jumps on the table. (when emphasizing movement to the surface)

Correct: The cat jumps onto the table.

Note: In casual English, "jump on the table" is also possible, but "onto" makes the movement clearer.

## 16. American English Notes

These prepositions are common in everyday American English.

Some useful American English expressions:

- Get in the car.
- Get out of the car.
- Get on the bus.
- Get off the bus.
- Walk across the street.
- Go down the block.
- Walk past the gas station.

In American English, **get in** is commonly used for cars, while **get on** is commonly used for buses and trains.

Examples:

- I get in my car at 8:00.
- She gets on the bus near her house.
- We get off the train downtown.

## 19. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- Prepositions of movement describe direction and paths.
- **To** indicates a destination.
- **From** indicates a starting point.
- **Into** describes movement from outside to inside.
- **Out of** describes movement from inside to outside.
- **Across** describes movement from one side to another.
- **Through** describes movement inside or through a passage or area.
- **Along** describes movement following a path.
- **Past** describes movement beyond something.
- **Up** and **down** describe vertical movement.
- **Onto** and **off** describe movement to and away from a surface.
