# Chapter 10: Prepositions of Place

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand common prepositions of place.
- Describe where people and objects are.
- Use in, on, and at correctly in basic situations.
- Use next to, between, behind, in front of, and under.
- Ask and answer questions about locations.

## 2. What Are Prepositions of Place?

Prepositions of place are words that describe where someone or something is located.

They help us answer the question:

**Where is it?**

Examples:

- The book is on the table.
- The cat is under the chair.
- My school is near the park.
- The bank is next to the supermarket.

## 3. In

Use **in** when something is inside an enclosed space, area, or container.

Examples:

- The keys are in my bag.
- The milk is in the refrigerator.
- She is in the kitchen.
- They live in New York.
- There is water in the bottle.

Common expressions:

- in the room
- in the house
- in the car
- in the kitchen
- in the city
- in the box

## 4. On

Use **on** when something is on a surface or attached to a surface.

Examples:

- The phone is on the table.
- There is a picture on the wall.
- The book is on the desk.
- The clock is on the wall.
- Your name is on the list.

Common expressions:

- on the table
- on the floor
- on the wall
- on the desk
- on the shelf

## 5. At

Use **at** to identify a specific point or location.

Examples:

- She is at the bus stop.
- I am at home.
- He is at school.
- We are at the airport.
- They are at the restaurant.

Common expressions:

- at home
- at school
- at work
- at the bus stop
- at the airport
- at the entrance

## 6. Comparison: In, On, and At

| Preposition | Basic Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| in | Inside an area or space | The dog is in the house. |
| on | On a surface | The cup is on the table. |
| at | At a specific point | She is at the door. |

Compare:

- He is **in** the school. (Inside the building.)
- He is **at** the school. (At that location; the focus is the place.)
- The picture is **on** the wall.
- The children are **in** the classroom.

## 7. Under

Use **under** when something is below another object.

Examples:

- The cat is under the table.
- My shoes are under the bed.
- The bag is under the chair.
- The ball is under the sofa.

## 8. Above and Below

Use **above** when something is at a higher level than another thing.

Examples:

- The clock is above the door.
- The picture is above the sofa.

Use **below** when something is at a lower level.

Examples:

- The temperature is below zero.
- The shelf is below the window.

## 9. Next To

Use **next to** when two people or things are very close to each other.

Examples:

- The bank is next to the supermarket.
- My house is next to a park.
- She is sitting next to her friend.
- The lamp is next to the bed.

## 10. Between

Use **between** when something is in the space separating two people or things.

Examples:

- The bank is between the hotel and the restaurant.
- The chair is between the two tables.
- I sit between my brother and my sister.

## 11. Behind

Use **behind** when something is at the back of another thing.

Examples:

- The car is behind the house.
- The garden is behind the building.
- The dog is behind the door.

## 12. In Front Of

Use **in front of** when something is at the forward side of another thing.

Examples:

- The bus is in front of the school.
- There is a tree in front of my house.
- She is standing in front of the door.

## 13. Near and Far From

Use **near** when something is close to another place or object.

Examples:

- My apartment is near the subway station.
- The school is near the park.
- There is a grocery store near my house.

Use **far from** when something is not close.

Examples:

- The airport is far from the city center.
- My office is far from my house.
- The hotel is far from the beach.

## 14. Opposite and Across From

Use **opposite** or **across from** to describe something on the other side of a space, street, or area.

Examples:

- The bank is opposite the supermarket.
- The restaurant is across from the hotel.
- The park is across from my apartment.

In everyday American English, **across from** is especially common.

## 15. Asking About Locations

Use **Where is...?** for one person or thing.

Examples:

- Where is my phone?
- Where is the bathroom?
- Where is the nearest bank?

Use **Where are...?** for plural people or things.

Examples:

- Where are my keys?
- Where are the students?
- Where are your shoes?

Possible answers:

- They are on the table.
- It is in my bag.
- The bathroom is next to the kitchen.
- The bank is across from the hotel.

## 16. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| The book is in the table. | The book is on the table. |
| My keys are on my bag. | My keys are in my bag. |
| She is in home. | She is at home. |
| The cat is under of the chair. | The cat is under the chair. |
| The bank is next the hotel. | The bank is next to the hotel. |
| The car is in front the house. | The car is in front of the house. |

## 17. American English Notes

- **Across from** is very common in American English for locations on the opposite side of a street or space.
- **At home** is the standard expression when talking about being at your home.
- **On the weekend** is common in American English when discussing time, although this chapter focuses on place.
- **Subway station** is commonly used in American English for an underground urban train station.

## 20. Chapter Summary

- **In** means inside an area or space.
- **On** means on a surface.
- **At** identifies a specific point or location.
- **Under** means below something.
- **Above** and **below** describe higher and lower positions.
- **Next to** means beside something.
- **Between** means in the space separating two things.
- **Behind** means at the back of something.
- **In front of** means at the forward side of something.
- **Near** and **far from** describe distance.
- **Across from** describes a location on the opposite side.
- Use **Where is...?** for singular nouns and **Where are...?** for plural nouns.
