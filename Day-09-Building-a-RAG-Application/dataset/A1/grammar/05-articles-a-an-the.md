# Chapter 5: Articles - A, An, and The

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, students will be able to:

- Understand definite and indefinite articles.
- Use a and an with singular countable nouns.
- Use the when referring to specific nouns.
- Understand when not to use an article.
- Choose articles based on pronunciation.
- Avoid common article mistakes.

## 2. What Are Articles?

Articles are small words that come before nouns.

English has three main articles:

- A
- An
- The

A and an are indefinite articles. They refer to a nonspecific person or thing.

The is a definite article. It refers to a specific person or thing.

Examples:

- I saw a dog.
- She ate an apple.
- The dog was very friendly.

## 3. Using A

Use "a" before a singular countable noun that begins with a consonant sound.

Examples:

- a book
- a car
- a teacher
- a house
- a university

Sentences:

- I have a car.
- She is a teacher.
- He lives in a house.
- I want a sandwich.

Important:

Choose a or an based on the first sound, not simply the first letter.

"University" begins with a /y/ sound, so we say "a university."

## 4. Using An

Use "an" before a singular countable noun that begins with a vowel sound.

Examples:

- an apple
- an egg
- an orange
- an umbrella
- an hour

Sentences:

- I ate an apple.
- She has an umbrella.
- He waited for an hour.

Important:

"Hour" begins with a vowel sound because the h is silent. Therefore, we say "an hour."

## 5. A vs. An

| A | An |
|---|---|
| a book | an apple |
| a car | an orange |
| a university | an umbrella |
| a European country | an hour |
| a useful tool | an honest person |

Remember:

The choice depends on pronunciation, not spelling.

## 6. Using The

Use "the" when the listener or reader knows which person or thing you mean.

### A. Something Already Mentioned

Example:

- I saw a dog.
- The dog was black.

We use "a" the first time and "the" when referring to the same dog again.

### B. Something Specific

Examples:

- Please close the door.
- Where is the bathroom?
- The teacher is in the classroom.

### C. Unique Things

Examples:

- the sun
- the moon
- the Earth
- the sky

### D. Superlative Adjectives

Examples:

- She is the tallest student.
- This is the best restaurant.
- He is the youngest person in the family.

## 7. A, An, and The: Comparison

| Article | Main Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A | Nonspecific singular countable noun before a consonant sound | a phone |
| An | Nonspecific singular countable noun before a vowel sound | an egg |
| The | Specific or known noun | the phone |

Compare:

- I need a pen. (Any pen)
- I need the pen on the table. (A specific pen)

- She wants an orange. (Any orange)
- She wants the orange in the fridge. (A specific orange)

## 8. When NOT to Use an Article

Sometimes English uses no article. This is called the zero article.

### A. General Plural Nouns

Examples:

- Dogs are friendly animals.
- Books are useful.
- Teachers help students.

### B. General Uncountable Nouns

Examples:

- Water is important.
- Music makes me happy.
- Information is useful.

### C. Most Personal Names

Examples:

- Sarah lives in California.
- John is my friend.
- Maria speaks English.

### D. Languages and School Subjects

Examples:

- I speak English.
- She studies mathematics.
- They learn Spanish.

### E. Meals in General

Examples:

- I eat breakfast at 8:00.
- We have lunch at noon.
- They eat dinner together.

## 9. Articles with Jobs and Professions

Use a or an before a singular job or profession.

Examples:

- I am a programmer.
- She is a doctor.
- He is an engineer.
- My father is a teacher.

Incorrect:
- She is doctor.

Correct:
- She is a doctor.

## 10. Common Mistakes

Incorrect: I have an car.

Correct: I have a car.

Incorrect: She is a engineer.

Correct: She is an engineer.

Incorrect: I saw dog.

Correct: I saw a dog.

Incorrect: The dogs are friendly. (When talking about dogs in general)

Correct: Dogs are friendly.

Incorrect: I speak the English.

Correct: I speak English.

Incorrect: He is doctor.

Correct: He is a doctor.

## 11. American English Notes

The basic rules for a, an, and the are the same in American and British English.

The pronunciation of "the" can vary slightly depending on the following sound and the speaker's accent.

Examples:

- the car
- the apple
- the United States

## 14. Chapter Summary

Remember:

- Use a before a singular countable noun beginning with a consonant sound.
- Use an before a singular countable noun beginning with a vowel sound.
- Use the for specific or already known nouns.
- Do not normally use articles with general plural or uncountable nouns.
- Use a or an before a singular job or profession.
- Choose a or an based on sound, not spelling.
