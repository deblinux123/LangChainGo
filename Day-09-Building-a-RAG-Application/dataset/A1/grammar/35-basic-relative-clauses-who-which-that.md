# Chapter 35 — Basic Relative Clauses: Who, Which, That

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand basic relative clauses.
- Use **who** to describe people.
- Use **which** to describe things and animals.
- Use **that** to describe people, things, and animals in defining relative clauses.
- Combine two simple sentences into one sentence.
- Recognize common mistakes.

## 2. What Is a Relative Clause?

A relative clause gives us more information about a person, animal, or thing.

We often use **who**, **which**, and **that** to introduce a relative clause.

Examples:

- I know a woman who speaks English.
- This is a book which teaches grammar.
- I have a dog that likes playing.

The relative clause describes the noun before it.

In the first sentence:

- Noun: a woman
- Relative clause: who speaks English

The relative clause tells us more about the woman.

## 3. Using WHO

Use **who** to describe people.

**Structure:**

`Person + who + verb + ...`

Examples:

- I have a friend who lives in New York.
- She knows a teacher who speaks Spanish.
- He is a man who works at a hospital.
- I like people who are kind.
- This is the girl who plays the piano.

More examples:

| Main Noun | Relative Clause |
|---|---|
| a teacher | who teaches English |
| a doctor | who works here |
| a friend | who lives nearby |
| a student | who studies hard |
| a woman | who speaks French |

## 4. Using WHICH

Use **which** to describe things and animals.

**Structure:**

`Thing or Animal + which + verb + ...`

Examples:

- This is a book which explains grammar.
- I have a phone which takes good pictures.
- She has a car which is very fast.
- We watched a movie which was funny.
- I have a cat which sleeps all day.

More examples:

| Main Noun | Relative Clause |
|---|---|
| a book | which teaches English |
| a phone | which takes pictures |
| a car | which is very expensive |
| a movie | which makes me laugh |
| a computer | which works well |

## 5. Using THAT

Use **that** to describe people, things, and animals in defining relative clauses.

A defining relative clause gives essential information about the noun.

Examples:

- I know a man that works here.
- This is the book that I bought.
- She has a dog that loves children.
- He likes movies that make him laugh.
- They live in a house that has a garden.

In these examples, **that** introduces information that helps identify or describe the noun.

## 6. WHO, WHICH, or THAT?

Use this simple guide:

| Relative Pronoun | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Who | People | A woman who works here |
| Which | Things and animals | A book which is interesting |
| That | People, things, and animals | A dog that likes children |

Examples:

- The boy who is running is my brother.
- The computer which is on the desk is mine.
- The girl that lives next door is friendly.
- The movie that we watched was interesting.

Remember:

- **Who** is used for people.
- **Which** is used for things and animals.
- **That** can be used for people, things, and animals in defining relative clauses.

## 7. Combining Two Sentences

Relative clauses help us combine two simple sentences.

### Example 1: Using WHO

Two sentences:

- I know a woman.
- She works at a bank.

Combined sentence:

- I know a woman who works at a bank.

### Example 2: Using WHICH

Two sentences:

- This is a phone.
- It has a good camera.

Combined sentence:

- This is a phone which has a good camera.

### Example 3: Using THAT

Two sentences:

- He has a dog.
- The dog loves children.

Combined sentence:

- He has a dog that loves children.

### More Examples

| Two Simple Sentences | Combined Sentence |
|---|---|
| I have a friend. He lives in Texas. | I have a friend who lives in Texas. |
| This is a book. It teaches English. | This is a book which teaches English. |
| She has a cat. The cat likes milk. | She has a cat that likes milk. |
| We know a teacher. She speaks French. | We know a teacher who speaks French. |

## 8. Relative Clauses with Objects

In some relative clauses, the relative pronoun is the object of the verb.

Examples:

- This is the book that I bought.
- She is the person who I called.
- This is the movie which we watched.

In everyday American English, **that** and **who** are common in these sentences.

In defining relative clauses, an object relative pronoun can sometimes be omitted:

- This is the book that I bought.
- This is the book I bought.

Both sentences are correct.

For now, focus on recognizing the complete forms with **who**, **which**, and **that**.

## 9. Important Grammar Rules

### Rule 1: Do not repeat the subject

Incorrect: I know a woman who she works here.

Correct: I know a woman who works here.

The word **who** already represents the woman.

### Rule 2: Use a verb after a subject relative pronoun

Correct:

- I have a friend who speaks English.
- This is a car that runs fast.

### Rule 3: Choose the correct relative pronoun

- People → who
- Things and animals → which
- People, things, and animals → that (in defining relative clauses)

### Rule 4: Do not add an extra subject after who or which

Incorrect: This is a phone which it has a good camera.

Correct: This is a phone which has a good camera.

## 10. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using which for a person

Incorrect: I know a man which works here.

Correct: I know a man who works here.

### Mistake 2: Using who for a thing

Incorrect: This is a book who teaches grammar.

Correct: This is a book which teaches grammar.

### Mistake 3: Repeating the subject

Incorrect: She has a dog that it likes milk.

Correct: She has a dog that likes milk.

### Mistake 4: Using an incorrect verb form

Incorrect: I know a teacher who teach English.

Correct: I know a teacher who teaches English.

Remember subject-verb agreement:

- A teacher who teaches
- Friends who teach

### Mistake 5: Confusing the noun and the relative clause

Incorrect: I have a friend who is live in Chicago.

Correct: I have a friend who lives in Chicago.

## 11. American English Notes

In American English:

- **Who** is commonly used for people.
- **Which** is commonly used for things and animals.
- **That** is very common in defining relative clauses.
- Object relative pronouns are often omitted in everyday conversation.

Examples:

- That's the guy who works with me.
- This is the laptop that I bought.
- She's the friend I told you about.

In formal writing, punctuation and the distinction between defining and non-defining clauses are important.

At this level, focus on simple defining relative clauses without commas.
