# Chapter 12: Can and Can't — Ability and Permission

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Use can to describe abilities.
- Use can't to describe things you are unable to do.
- Ask and answer questions with can.
- Use can to make simple requests and ask for permission.
- Understand the basic structure of sentences with modal verbs.

## 2. What Is Can?

**Can** is a modal verb. We commonly use it to talk about:

1. Ability: something a person is able to do.
2. Permission: something a person is allowed to do.
3. Requests: asking someone to do something.

Examples:

- I can swim.
- She can speak English.
- Can I use your phone?
- Can you help me?

## 3. Positive Sentences

Use **can** before the base form of a verb.

Structure:

**Subject + can + base verb + ...**

Examples:

- I can drive.
- She can cook.
- He can play the guitar.
- We can speak English.
- They can run fast.

### Important Rules

After can, always use the base form of the verb.

Correct:

- She can swim.
- He can speak English.
- They can play soccer.

Incorrect:

- She can swims.
- He can speaks English.
- They can to play soccer.

The verb does not change with he, she, or it.

## 4. Negative Sentences: Cannot and Can't

Use **cannot** or its contraction **can't** to express inability or lack of permission.

Structure:

**Subject + cannot/can't + base verb + ...**

Examples:

- I can't swim.
- She can't drive.
- He cannot speak French.
- We can't come today.
- They can't play the piano.

Both cannot and can't are correct.

In everyday American English, can't is very common.

## 5. Questions with Can

To make a question, put **can** before the subject.

Structure:

**Can + subject + base verb + ...?**

Examples:

- Can you swim?
- Can she drive?
- Can they speak English?
- Can he play basketball?
- Can we start now?

### Short Answers

| Question | Positive Answer | Negative Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Can you swim? | Yes, I can. | No, I can't. |
| Can she drive? | Yes, she can. | No, she can't. |
| Can they cook? | Yes, they can. | No, they can't. |
| Can he speak English? | Yes, he can. | No, he can't. |

Do not repeat the main verb in a short answer.

Correct:

- Yes, I can.
- No, she can't.

## 6. Can for Ability

We use can to describe skills and abilities.

Examples:

- I can speak two languages.
- My brother can play the guitar.
- She can cook very well.
- He can run five miles.
- They can use a computer.

We use can't when someone does not have an ability.

Examples:

- I can't play the piano.
- He can't swim.
- She can't speak Japanese.

## 7. Can for Permission

We can use can to ask for or give permission.

Examples:

- Can I use your computer?
- Can I open the window?
- Can we sit here?
- You can use my phone.
- You can't park here.

In everyday American English, can is common for asking permission.

## 8. Can for Requests

We use can to ask someone to do something.

Structure:

**Can + subject + base verb + ...?**

Examples:

- Can you help me?
- Can you open the door?
- Can you repeat that?
- Can you speak more slowly?
- Can you call me later?

Common responses:

- Sure.
- Of course.
- No problem.
- Sorry, I can't.
- I'm sorry, I'm busy right now.

## 9. The Verb After Can

The verb after can always stays in its base form.

| Subject | Correct Sentence |
|---|---|
| I | I can work. |
| You | You can work. |
| He | He can work. |
| She | She can work. |
| It | It can work. |
| We | We can work. |
| They | They can work. |

Remember:

- Do not add -s to the verb.
- Do not use to after can.
- Do not use do or does to form questions with can.

## 10. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| She can speaks English. | She can speak English. |
| He can to drive. | He can drive. |
| Do you can swim? | Can you swim? |
| I don't can cook. | I can't cook. |
| Can she sings? | Can she sing? |
| He cans play soccer. | He can play soccer. |

## 11. American English Notes

- Can't is very common in everyday American conversations.
- Can is frequently used for informal requests and permission.
- "Can you give me a hand?" is a common informal request for help.
- "Can I get a glass of water?" is a natural way to ask for water in a restaurant or other casual setting.

## 14. Chapter Summary

- Can is a modal verb used for ability, permission, and requests.
- Use can + base verb in positive sentences.
- Use cannot or can't in negative sentences.
- Put can before the subject to form questions.
- Use short answers such as Yes, I can and No, I can't.
- Never add -s or -es to a verb after can.
- Never use to after can.
- Do not use do or does to form questions with can.
