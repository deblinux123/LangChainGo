# Chapter 23 — Adverbs of Frequency

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand adverbs of frequency.
- Talk about how often you do everyday activities.
- Use common frequency adverbs in sentences.
- Put frequency adverbs in the correct position.
- Ask and answer questions with "How often?"

## 2. What Are Adverbs of Frequency?

Adverbs of frequency tell us how often something happens.

They describe whether an action happens all the time, often, sometimes, or never.

Common adverbs of frequency include:

| Adverb | Meaning | Approximate Frequency |
|---|---|---:|
| Always | Every time | 100% |
| Usually | Most of the time | 80–90% |
| Normally | In most situations | 80–90% |
| Often | Many times | 60–70% |
| Sometimes | On some occasions | 40–50% |
| Occasionally | Not very often | 20–30% |
| Rarely | Almost never | 5–10% |
| Hardly ever | Almost never | 1–5% |
| Never | Not at any time | 0% |

These percentages are approximate. They help you understand the general meaning, not exact mathematical rules.

## 3. The Frequency Scale

We can arrange frequency adverbs from most frequent to least frequent.

**Most frequent**

1. Always
2. Usually
3. Normally
4. Often
5. Sometimes
6. Occasionally
7. Rarely
8. Hardly ever
9. Never

**Least frequent**

Examples:

- I always brush my teeth.
- She usually walks to work.
- We often watch movies.
- They sometimes eat pizza.
- He rarely drinks coffee.
- I never smoke.

## 4. Position in a Sentence

The position of an adverb of frequency depends on the verb.

### Rule 1: Before Most Main Verbs

Place the adverb before the main verb.

**Structure:**

Subject + frequency adverb + main verb + ...

Examples:

- I always drink coffee.
- She usually gets up early.
- They often play soccer.
- We sometimes cook dinner.
- He rarely watches TV.
- My parents never eat fast food.

More examples:

| Subject | Adverb | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| I | always | study English. |
| She | usually | walks to work. |
| We | often | eat together. |
| They | sometimes | visit us. |
| He | never | drives to work. |

### Rule 2: After the Verb "To Be"

When the main verb is a form of **be** (am, is, are), place the frequency adverb after it.

**Structure:**

Subject + am/is/are + frequency adverb + ...

Examples:

- I am always busy.
- She is usually happy.
- They are often tired.
- He is sometimes late.
- We are never angry.

Compare:

- I always am busy. (Not the usual word order.)
- I am always busy. (Natural word order.)

### Rule 3: With Modal Verbs

Place the frequency adverb after the modal verb and before the main verb.

Examples:

- I can always help you.
- She can usually come early.
- We should often practice English.
- You must never touch this machine.

At A1 level, focus mainly on the first two rules.

## 5. Negative Sentences

We can use frequency adverbs in negative sentences.

Examples:

- I don't usually drink tea.
- She doesn't often watch TV.
- They don't always eat breakfast.
- He isn't usually late.
- We aren't always busy.

Notice the position:

- With don't or doesn't, the adverb usually comes after the auxiliary verb.
- With am, is, or are, the adverb usually comes after the verb be.

## 6. Questions with How Often?

Use **How often** to ask about the frequency of an activity.

### Present Simple Questions

**Structure:**

How often + do/does + subject + base verb + ...?

Examples:

- How often do you exercise?
- How often do they visit their grandparents?
- How often does she cook dinner?
- How often does he play soccer?

### Answers

You can answer with an adverb of frequency.

Examples:

**Question:** How often do you read books?

- I always read books.
- I usually read books.
- I sometimes read books.
- I rarely read books.
- I never read books.

**Question:** How often does she go shopping?

**Answer:** She usually goes shopping on Saturdays.

## 7. Other Ways to Talk About Frequency

You can also describe frequency using expressions such as:

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| Every day | I study English every day. |
| Every morning | She exercises every morning. |
| Every weekend | We visit our friends every weekend. |
| Once a week | I play soccer once a week. |
| Twice a week | He goes to the gym twice a week. |
| Three times a month | They eat at restaurants three times a month. |
| Once a year | We travel abroad once a year. |

### Once, Twice, and Times

- Once = one time
- Twice = two times
- Three times = three times
- Four times = four times

Examples:

- I call my parents once a day.
- She takes English classes twice a week.
- We visit the dentist twice a year.

## 8. Adverbs vs. Frequency Expressions

Adverbs of frequency and frequency expressions can both describe how often something happens.

Compare:

- I usually exercise.
- I exercise every day.
- She sometimes cooks dinner.
- She cooks dinner twice a week.

Frequency adverbs usually go before the main verb or after the verb be.

Frequency expressions such as "every day" and "twice a week" commonly go at the end of a sentence.

## 9. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Wrong position with main verbs

Incorrect: I drink always coffee.

Correct: I always drink coffee.

### Mistake 2: Wrong position with "be"

Incorrect: She always is happy.

Correct: She is always happy.

### Mistake 3: Using "never" with another negative

Incorrect: I don't never drink coffee.

Correct: I never drink coffee.

### Mistake 4: Forgetting the -s with he, she, and it

Incorrect: He usually go to work.

Correct: He usually goes to work.

### Mistake 5: Wrong question structure

Incorrect: How often you exercise?

Correct: How often do you exercise?

### Mistake 6: Using "twice times"

Incorrect: I go there twice times a week.

Correct: I go there twice a week.

## 10. American English Notes

- **Always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, and never** are common in everyday American English.
- Americans frequently use "a lot" in conversation to describe frequency.
- "All the time" is a common informal expression meaning very frequently.
- "Hardly ever" is a natural way to say that something almost never happens.

Examples:

- I go there a lot.
- He talks on the phone all the time.
- We hardly ever eat out.
- She usually gets coffee before work.

Remember: "A lot" usually comes after the verb or object in these sentences, not before the main verb.
