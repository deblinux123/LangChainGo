# Chapter 39: Basic Sentence Connectors and Compound Sentences

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand why we use sentence connectors.
- Connect words, phrases, and simple sentences.
- Use "and" to add information.
- Use "but" to show contrast.
- Use "or" to give choices.
- Use "because" to give reasons.
- Use "so" to show results.
- Use "when" to connect actions and time.
- Build basic compound sentences.
- Use commas correctly in simple compound sentences.

---

## 2. What Are Sentence Connectors?

Sentence connectors are words that join ideas, words, or sentences.

They help us make our communication clearer and more natural.

Compare:

- I like coffee. I like tea.
- I like coffee and tea.

The second sentence connects two ideas and sounds more natural.

### Common Sentence Connectors

| Connector | Main Use |
|---|---|
| And | Adds information |
| But | Shows contrast |
| Or | Gives choices |
| Because | Gives a reason |
| So | Shows a result |
| When | Connects actions with time |

---

## 3. Using "And"

Use "and" to connect similar ideas or add information.

### 3.1 Connecting Nouns

Examples:

- I like coffee and tea.
- She has a brother and a sister.
- We need bread and milk.
- He speaks English and Spanish.

### 3.2 Connecting Adjectives

Examples:

- The room is clean and comfortable.
- My teacher is friendly and helpful.
- The movie is funny and interesting.

### 3.3 Connecting Verbs

Examples:

- I wake up and take a shower.
- She cooks and cleans.
- They sing and dance.
- We study and practice English.

### 3.4 Connecting Sentences

Examples:

- I like music, and my brother likes movies.
- She works in a hospital, and her husband works in a school.
- We live in Chicago, and our parents live in Boston.

**Punctuation:** When "and" joins two complete sentences, a comma is commonly used before "and."

---

## 4. Using "But"

Use "but" to connect two ideas that are different or contrast with each other.

### Examples

- I like coffee, but I don't like tea.
- She is tired, but she is happy.
- He is young, but he is very responsible.
- The house is small, but it is comfortable.
- They speak English, but they don't speak French.

### Compare "And" and "But"

**And:** Adds similar or related information.

- I like pizza, and I like burgers.

**But:** Shows a difference or contrast.

- I like pizza, but I don't like burgers.

### More Examples

- It is cold, but I am not wearing a jacket.
- She is busy, but she can help us.
- He works hard, but he doesn't earn much money.

---

## 5. Using "Or"

Use "or" to give choices or alternatives.

### 5.1 Connecting Nouns

Examples:

- Do you want coffee or tea?
- Is your car black or white?
- Would you like an apple or a banana?

### 5.2 Connecting Verbs

Examples:

- We can walk or take a bus.
- You can call me or send me a message.
- They can stay or leave.

### 5.3 Connecting Sentences

Examples:

- We can watch a movie, or we can play a game.
- You can study now, or you can study later.
- We can eat at home, or we can go to a restaurant.

### Negative Choices with "Or"

Examples:

- I don't drink coffee or tea.
- She doesn't eat meat or fish.

**Remember:** In many negative sentences, "or" connects the things that are not included.

---

## 6. Using "Because"

Use "because" to explain why something happens.

**Structure:**

Main Idea + Because + Reason

Examples:

- I am happy because it is my birthday.
- She is tired because she works a lot.
- He stays home because he is sick.
- We study English because we want to travel.
- They are late because the bus is slow.

### Because at the Beginning

You can also put the reason first.

**Structure:**

Because + Reason + Main Idea

Examples:

- Because I am tired, I want to sleep.
- Because it is raining, we are staying home.
- Because she is sick, she isn't at work.

When the "because" clause comes first, use a comma before the main clause.

### Common Mistake

Incorrect: I am happy because of I have a new job.

Correct: I am happy because I have a new job.

Use "because" before a complete clause:

- because I am tired
- because she is busy
- because they need help

Use "because of" before a noun or noun phrase:

- because of the rain
- because of my job
- because of the traffic

---

## 7. Using "So"

Use "so" to show the result of something.

**Structure:**

Reason or Situation + So + Result

Examples:

- I am tired, so I want to sleep.
- It is raining, so we are staying home.
- She is hungry, so she is making a sandwich.
- He is sick, so he isn't going to work.
- The store is closed, so we can't buy anything.

### Because vs. So

"Because" introduces a reason.

"So" introduces a result.

Compare:

- I stayed home because I was sick.
- I was sick, so I stayed home.

Both sentences express the same general relationship, but the information is presented in a different order.

### Common Mistake

Incorrect: I am tired, because I want to sleep. (This changes the intended meaning if sleep is the result of being tired.)

Better: I am tired, so I want to sleep.

---

## 8. Using "When"

Use "when" to connect an action with a time or another action.

### 8.1 When at the Beginning

**Structure:**

When + Time Clause, + Main Clause

Examples:

- When I get home, I watch TV.
- When she is hungry, she eats fruit.
- When it rains, we stay inside.
- When he finishes work, he goes home.

### 8.2 When in the Middle

Examples:

- I watch TV when I get home.
- She calls me when she needs help.
- We eat dinner when our father comes home.

### Important Note

Use the present simple to talk about regular habits or general situations.

Examples:

- When I wake up, I drink water.
- When she gets home, she calls her mother.

At A1 level, focus on using "when" to describe simple routines and familiar situations.

---

## 9. What Is a Compound Sentence?

A compound sentence connects two independent clauses.

An independent clause is a group of words that can stand alone as a complete sentence.

Examples of independent clauses:

- I like music.
- She works at a hospital.
- They live in New York.
- We study English.

You can connect two independent clauses with a coordinating connector.

### Examples

- I like music, and she likes movies.
- He is tired, but he is working.
- We can walk, or we can take a bus.
- It is cold, so I am wearing a coat.

### Basic Structure

Independent Clause + Connector + Independent Clause

A comma is commonly used before "and," "but," or "or" when they connect two complete independent clauses.

Examples:

- I am hungry, so I am making lunch.
- She likes dogs, but she doesn't like cats.
- We can stay here, or we can go home.

**Important:** Do not use a comma automatically when a connector joins only words or short phrases.

Correct: I like apples and oranges.

Correct: I like apples, and my sister likes oranges.

## 10. Choosing the Correct Connector

Use the meaning of the sentence to choose the connector.

| Meaning | Connector | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Add information | And | I like tea and coffee. |
| Show contrast | But | I am tired, but I am happy. |
| Give a choice | Or | Do you want water or juice? |
| Give a reason | Because | I am home because I am sick. |
| Show a result | So | I am sick, so I am home. |
| Connect time | When | I smile when I see you. |

### Compare These Sentences

1. I am hungry, and I want a sandwich.
   - Adds another related idea.

2. I am hungry, but I don't want to eat.
   - Shows contrast.

3. Do you want a sandwich or a salad?
   - Gives a choice.

4. I want a sandwich because I am hungry.
   - Gives a reason.

5. I am hungry, so I want a sandwich.
   - Shows a result.

## 11. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| I like tea but coffee. | I like tea and coffee. |
| I am tired because I go to bed. | I am tired, so I want to go to bed. |
| I stayed home so I was sick. | I stayed home because I was sick. |
| Because I am tired. | I am going to sleep because I am tired. |
| I like apples, and oranges. | I like apples and oranges. |
| She is busy, because she works. | She is busy because she works. |
| I want coffee or I want tea? | Do you want coffee or tea? |
| When I get home. | I watch TV when I get home. |

### Remember

- "And" adds information.
- "But" shows contrast.
- "Or" gives alternatives.
- "Because" gives a reason.
- "So" gives a result.
- "When" connects ideas with time.

## 12. American English Notes

These connectors are used in both American and British English.

In everyday American English, speakers often use short and direct sentences.

Examples:

- I'm tired, so I'm going home.
- I like pizza, but I don't like mushrooms.
- Do you want coffee or tea?
- I'm staying home because it's raining.
- I call my mom when I get home.

### Contractions in Connected Sentences

Contractions are common in informal American English.

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| I am | I'm |
| She is | She's |
| Do not | Don't |
| Does not | Doesn't |
| Cannot | Can't |
| It is | It's |
| We are | We're |
| They are | They're |

Examples:

- I'm tired, so I'm going to bed.
- She doesn't drive because she lives near work.
- It's cold, but we're going outside.

## 15. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned how to connect simple English ideas and sentences.

Key points:

- Use "and" to add information.
- Use "but" to show contrast.
- Use "or" to give choices.
- Use "because" to explain reasons.
- Use "so" to show results.
- Use "when" to connect actions with time.
- A compound sentence joins two independent clauses.
- A comma is commonly used before "and," "but," or "or" when they connect two complete independent clauses.
- Do not use a comma automatically when connecting only words or short phrases.

**Final Reminder:** Choose a connector based on the relationship between your ideas. Practice with short sentences before writing longer paragraphs.
