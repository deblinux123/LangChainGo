# Chapter 34 — Gerunds and Infinitives: Basic Verbs

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand what gerunds are.
- Understand what infinitives are.
- Use verbs followed by gerunds.
- Use verbs followed by infinitives.
- Use gerunds and infinitives in simple sentences.
- Recognize common mistakes.

## 2. What Is a Gerund?

A gerund is a verb ending in **-ing** that works like a noun.

Examples:

- swimming
- reading
- cooking
- dancing
- learning
- traveling

We can use gerunds as the subject of a sentence.

Examples:

- Swimming is fun.
- Reading is important.
- Cooking is easy.
- Learning English takes time.

We can also use gerunds after certain verbs.

Examples:

- I enjoy reading.
- She likes dancing.
- He loves cooking.
- They finish working at five.

## 3. What Is an Infinitive?

An infinitive is the basic form of a verb with **to**.

**Structure:**

`to + base form of the verb`

Examples:

- to read
- to swim
- to cook
- to study
- to travel
- to learn

We often use infinitives after certain verbs.

Examples:

- I want to learn English.
- She needs to study.
- He hopes to travel.
- They plan to visit us.

## 4. Gerund vs. Infinitive

Compare these structures:

| Gerund | Infinitive |
|---|---|
| reading | to read |
| swimming | to swim |
| cooking | to cook |
| studying | to study |
| traveling | to travel |

Examples:

- I enjoy reading.
- I want to read.
- She likes swimming.
- She wants to swim.
- He loves cooking.
- He needs to cook.

Remember:

- A gerund ends in **-ing**.
- An infinitive uses **to + base verb**.

## 5. Verbs Followed by Gerunds

Some common verbs are followed by gerunds.

### Enjoy + Gerund

Examples:

- I enjoy reading.
- She enjoys cooking.
- They enjoy playing soccer.
- We enjoy learning English.

### Like + Gerund

Examples:

- I like swimming.
- He likes listening to music.
- They like watching movies.
- She likes dancing.

### Love + Gerund

Examples:

- I love traveling.
- She loves singing.
- He loves playing basketball.
- We love spending time together.

### Hate + Gerund

Examples:

- I hate waiting.
- She hates cleaning.
- He hates getting up early.
- They hate doing homework.

### Finish + Gerund

Examples:

- I finished eating.
- She finished studying.
- We finished cleaning the room.
- He finished writing the email.

## 6. Verbs Followed by Infinitives

Some common verbs are followed by infinitives.

### Want + Infinitive

Examples:

- I want to learn English.
- She wants to buy a car.
- He wants to travel.
- They want to watch a movie.

### Need + Infinitive

Examples:

- I need to study.
- She needs to sleep.
- We need to work.
- He needs to call his mother.

### Hope + Infinitive

Examples:

- I hope to see you.
- She hopes to pass the test.
- They hope to visit New York.
- We hope to win.

### Plan + Infinitive

Examples:

- I plan to study tonight.
- He plans to buy a computer.
- They plan to travel next summer.
- We plan to visit our friends.

### Decide + Infinitive

Examples:

- I decided to stay home.
- She decided to learn Spanish.
- He decided to change his job.
- They decided to leave early.

## 7. Verbs That Can Use Both

Some verbs can be followed by either a gerund or an infinitive.

At this level, focus on the common verbs **like, love, and hate**.

Examples:

- I like reading.
- I like to read.
- She loves dancing.
- She loves to dance.
- He hates waiting.
- He hates to wait.

These forms often have similar meanings.

In everyday English, both structures are common.

## 8. Gerunds After Prepositions

Use a gerund after a preposition when a verb follows it.

**Structure:**

`Preposition + verb-ing`

Examples:

- I am interested in learning English.
- She is good at cooking.
- He is afraid of flying.
- Thank you for helping me.
- We talked about moving to another city.

More common expressions:

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| good at | She is good at drawing. |
| interested in | I am interested in learning. |
| afraid of | He is afraid of swimming. |
| tired of | I am tired of waiting. |
| thank you for | Thank you for helping us. |

Incorrect: She is good at cook.

Correct: She is good at cooking.

## 9. Common Sentence Patterns

### Pattern 1: Subject + Verb + Gerund

- I enjoy reading.
- She likes dancing.
- They finished working.

### Pattern 2: Subject + Verb + Infinitive

- I want to sleep.
- He needs to study.
- We decided to leave.

### Pattern 3: Subject + Be + Adjective + Preposition + Gerund

- She is good at singing.
- I am interested in learning.
- He is afraid of driving.

### Pattern 4: Gerund as the Subject

- Running is healthy.
- Cooking is fun.
- Learning English is useful.

## 10. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using the base verb after enjoy

Incorrect: I enjoy read.

Correct: I enjoy reading.

### Mistake 2: Forgetting to after want

Incorrect: I want learn English.

Correct: I want to learn English.

### Mistake 3: Using a gerund after need

Incorrect: She needs studying.

Correct: She needs to study.

### Mistake 4: Using the base verb after a preposition

Incorrect: He is good at cook.

Correct: He is good at cooking.

### Mistake 5: Using two verbs without the correct structure

Incorrect: I decided going home.

Correct: I decided to go home.

### Mistake 6: Adding to before a gerund

Incorrect: I enjoy to reading.

Correct: I enjoy reading.

## 11. American English Notes

Gerunds and infinitives are used in standard American English.

Common American English examples:

- I love watching TV.
- I want to grab some coffee.
- She enjoys hanging out with friends.
- We need to get going.
- He is interested in learning new skills.

**Get going** is an informal expression meaning to start leaving or start doing something.

Example:

- It's late. We need to get going.

## 14. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- A gerund is a verb ending in **-ing** that can work as a noun.
- An infinitive is **to + base form of a verb**.
- Common verbs followed by gerunds include enjoy, finish, and like.
- Common verbs followed by infinitives include want, need, hope, plan, and decide.
- Like, love, and hate can be followed by either form in many common sentences.
- Use gerunds after prepositions when a verb follows.

### Quick Review

|---|---|

| want + infinitive | I want to learn. |
| need + infinitive | He needs to sleep. |
| plan + infinitive | We plan to travel. |
| preposition + gerund | She is good at dancing. |
