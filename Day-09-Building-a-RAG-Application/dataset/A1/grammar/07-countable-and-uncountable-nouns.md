# Chapter 07: Countable and Uncountable Nouns

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand the difference between countable and uncountable nouns.
- Use singular and plural forms of countable nouns.
- Use uncountable nouns correctly.
- Use basic quantifiers such as some, any, much, many, and a lot of.
- Ask questions using How much and How many.
- Use containers and measurement expressions.

## 2. What Are Countable Nouns?

Countable nouns are nouns that we can count individually.

They have singular and plural forms.

Examples:

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| one apple | two apples |
| one book | three books |
| one chair | four chairs |
| one student | five students |
| one computer | two computers |

We can use numbers with countable nouns.

Examples:

- I have one brother.
- She has three cats.
- There are ten students in the classroom.
- He bought two sandwiches.

### Important Rule

Use a or an with singular countable nouns.

Correct:

- I have a car.
- She eats an apple.
- He is a teacher.

Incorrect:

- I have car.
- She eats apple.

## 3. What Are Uncountable Nouns?

Uncountable nouns are things that we do not normally count individually.

They usually do not have a plural form.

Examples:

| Category | Uncountable Nouns |
|---|---|
| Food and drinks | water, milk, rice, sugar, coffee |
| Materials | wood, plastic, glass, paper |
| Abstract ideas | love, happiness, information, advice |
| Other | money, furniture, homework, traffic |

Examples:

- I drink water every day.
- She needs some information.
- We have furniture in our apartment.
- He drinks coffee in the morning.
- I have a lot of homework.

### Important Rules

Do not normally use a or an directly before an uncountable noun.

Correct:

- I need water.
- She wants some coffee.
- We need information.

Incorrect:

- I need a water.
- She wants a coffee. (When referring to coffee as a substance.)

Note: In American English, "a coffee" is common when ordering one serving or one cup of coffee.

## 4. Countable vs. Uncountable

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| apple | water |
| book | rice |
| chair | furniture |
| student | information |
| egg | sugar |
| sandwich | bread |
| idea | advice |
| car | money |

Compare:

- I have three apples.
- I have some water.
- She bought two books.
- She bought some furniture.
- We need an egg.
- We need some rice.

## 5. Using Some

We commonly use some in positive sentences with plural countable nouns and uncountable nouns.

Structure:

- Some + plural countable noun
- Some + uncountable noun

Examples:

- I have some friends.
- There are some books on the table.
- We need some milk.
- She bought some rice.
- They have some questions.

We also use some in offers and requests when we expect or invite a positive response.

Examples:

- Would you like some coffee?
- Can I have some water?

## 6. Using Any

We commonly use any in negative sentences and questions.

Examples:

- I don't have any money.
- There aren't any students here.
- Do you have any questions?
- Is there any milk in the refrigerator?

Compare:

| Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|
| I have some books. | I don't have any books. | Do you have any books? |
| We need some water. | We don't need any water. | Do we need any water? |

## 7. Using Much and Many

Use many with plural countable nouns.

Examples:

- How many books do you have?
- There are many people here.
- She doesn't have many friends.

Use much with uncountable nouns.

Examples:

- How much water do you drink?
- I don't have much money.
- Is there much sugar in your coffee?

In everyday American English, a lot of is often more natural than much in positive statements.

Examples:

- I have a lot of books.
- She drinks a lot of water.

## 8. Using A Lot Of

We can use a lot of with both plural countable and uncountable nouns.

Examples:

- I have a lot of friends.
- She has a lot of books.
- We drink a lot of water.
- They have a lot of homework.

Structure:

- A lot of + plural countable noun
- A lot of + uncountable noun

## 9. How Much and How Many

Use How many to ask about plural countable nouns.

Structure:

How many + plural countable noun + ...?

Examples:

- How many brothers do you have?
- How many books are there?
- How many students are in your class?

Use How much to ask about uncountable nouns.

Structure:

How much + uncountable noun + ...?

Examples:

- How much water do you drink?
- How much money do you need?
- How much sugar is in your tea?

We also use How much to ask about prices.

Examples:

- How much is this shirt?
- How much are these shoes?

## 10. Containers and Units

We can use containers or measurement expressions to count uncountable nouns.

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| a glass of water | I want a glass of water. |
| a bottle of milk | She bought a bottle of milk. |
| a cup of coffee | He drinks a cup of coffee. |
| a piece of bread | I ate a piece of bread. |
| a bowl of rice | She wants a bowl of rice. |
| a piece of advice | Let me give you a piece of advice. |
| a piece of information | I found a useful piece of information. |
| a bag of sugar | We need a bag of sugar. |

The container or unit is countable, even when the noun after of is uncountable.

Examples:

- one bottle of water
- two bottles of water
- three pieces of advice

## 11. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| I need an information. | I need some information. |
| She gave me two advices. | She gave me two pieces of advice. |
| I have many money. | I have a lot of money. |
| There are much apples. | There are many apples. |
| I bought three furniture. | I bought three pieces of furniture. |
| He drinks a water. | He drinks some water. |

Remember:

- Information is uncountable.
- Advice is uncountable.
- Furniture is uncountable.
- Homework is uncountable.
- Money is uncountable.

## 12. American English Notes

- "A coffee" is commonly used to mean one serving of coffee.
- "A glass of water" and "a bottle of water" are common everyday expressions.
- "A lot of" is very common in everyday American English.
- "Information," "advice," and "furniture" are uncountable in standard American English.

## 15. Chapter Summary

- Countable nouns can be counted and have singular and plural forms.
- Uncountable nouns are not normally counted individually and usually have no plural form.
- Use a or an with singular countable nouns.
- Use some in many positive sentences.
- Use any in many negative sentences and questions.
- Use many with plural countable nouns.
- Use much with uncountable nouns.
- Use a lot of with both countable and uncountable nouns.
- Use How many for countable nouns and How much for uncountable nouns.
- Use containers and units to express quantities of uncountable nouns.
