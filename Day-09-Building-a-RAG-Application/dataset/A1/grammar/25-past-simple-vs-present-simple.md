# Chapter 25 — Past Simple vs. Present Simple

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand the difference between the past simple and present simple.
- Talk about daily routines and past activities.
- Describe completed actions in the past.
- Choose the correct tense using time expressions.
- Make positive, negative, and question sentences in both tenses.
- Compare past habits and present routines.

## 2. Present Simple: Habits and Facts

We use the **present simple** to talk about:

- Daily routines.
- Regular activities.
- Habits.
- Facts and general truths.
- Things that are generally true now.

### Examples

- I work in an office.
- She drinks coffee every morning.
- They play soccer on weekends.
- He lives in Chicago.
- We study English every day.

### Structure

**Positive:**

Subject + base verb (+ s/es for he, she, it) + ...

Examples:

- I work every day.
- You study English.
- He works every day.
- She studies English.
- They live here.

**Negative:**

Subject + don't / doesn't + base verb + ...

Examples:

- I don't work on Sundays.
- She doesn't drink coffee.
- They don't play tennis.
- He doesn't watch TV.

**Questions:**

Do / Does + subject + base verb + ...?

Examples:

- Do you work every day?
- Does she speak English?
- Do they live here?
- Does he play soccer?

## 3. Past Simple: Completed Actions in the Past

We use the **past simple** to talk about actions and events that started and finished in the past.

Examples:

- I worked yesterday.
- She visited her grandmother last weekend.
- They played soccer two days ago.
- He watched a movie last night.
- We studied English yesterday.

The action is finished. It is not happening now.

### Structure

**Positive:**

Subject + past form of the verb + ...

Examples:

- I worked yesterday.
- She visited her friend.
- He went to school.
- They ate dinner.
- We saw a movie.

Regular verbs usually end in **-ed** in the past simple. Irregular verbs have different forms.

## 4. The Main Difference

The main difference is the time of the action.

- **Present simple:** A habit, routine, or general fact.
- **Past simple:** A completed action or event in the past.

| Present Simple | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| I work every day. | I worked yesterday. |
| She drinks coffee every morning. | She drank coffee this morning. |
| They play soccer on Sundays. | They played soccer last Sunday. |
| He goes to school by bus. | He went to school by bus yesterday. |
| We watch movies on weekends. | We watched a movie last night. |

### Compare These Situations

**Situation 1: Work**

- I work in a bank now.
- I worked in a restaurant last year.

**Situation 2: Exercise**

- She exercises every morning.
- She exercised yesterday morning.

**Situation 3: Travel**

- They travel every summer.
- They traveled to New York last summer.

**Situation 4: Study**

- He studies English every day.
- He studied English last night.

## 5. Time Expressions

Time expressions help us understand when an action happens.

### Present Simple

Common expressions:

- Every day
- Every morning
- Every week
- On Mondays
- Usually
- Always
- Often
- Sometimes
- Never

Examples:

- I usually wake up at 7:00.
- She goes to the gym every week.
- They often visit their friends.
- He never drinks soda.

### Past Simple

Common expressions:

- Yesterday
- Yesterday morning
- Last night
- Last week
- Last month
- Last year
- Two days ago
- Three weeks ago
- In 2020
- When I was young

Examples:

- I called my friend yesterday.
- She traveled last month.
- They moved here two years ago.
- We visited our grandparents last weekend.
- He graduated in 2020.

## 6. Negative Sentences in the Past Simple

Use **didn't** (did not) to make negative sentences in the past simple.

**Structure:**

Subject + didn't + base verb + ...

Examples:

- I didn't work yesterday.
- She didn't visit her friend.
- They didn't play soccer.
- He didn't go to school.
- We didn't eat breakfast.

Important: After **didn't**, use the base form of the verb.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| She didn't went. | She didn't go. |
| He didn't worked. | He didn't work. |
| They didn't played. | They didn't play. |
| I didn't saw him. | I didn't see him. |

## 7. Questions in the Past Simple

Use **did** to ask questions about completed past actions.

**Structure:**

Did + subject + base verb + ...?

Examples:

- Did you work yesterday?
- Did she call you?
- Did they visit the museum?
- Did he go to school?
- Did you watch the movie?

### Short Answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Did you work yesterday? | Yes, I did. |
| Did she call you? | No, she didn't. |
| Did they play soccer? | Yes, they did. |
| Did he go home? | No, he didn't. |

Important: After **did**, use the base form of the verb.

Incorrect: Did you went there?

Correct: Did you go there?

## 8. Present Simple vs. Past Simple: Questions

Compare how we ask about routines and past events.

| Present Simple | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| Do you work here? | Did you work here last year? |
| Does she live in Boston? | Did she live in Boston before? |
| Do they play soccer? | Did they play soccer yesterday? |
| Does he drive to work? | Did he drive to work yesterday? |

Examples:

- Do you study English every day?
- Did you study English yesterday?
- Does she cook dinner every night?
- Did she cook dinner last night?

## 9. The Verb "Be": Present and Past

The verb **be** has different forms in the present and past.

| Subject | Present | Past |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | was |
| He / She / It | is | was |
| You / We / They | are | were |

Examples:

- I am tired today.
- I was tired yesterday.
- She is at home now.
- She was at home last night.
- They are happy today.
- They were happy yesterday.

### Negative Forms

Present:

- I am not tired.
- She isn't at home.
- They aren't ready.

Past:

- I wasn't tired.
- She wasn't at home.
- They weren't ready.

### Questions

Present:

- Are you happy?
- Is she at home?

Past:

- Were you happy?
- Was she at home?

Remember: With the past form of **be**, we do not use did.

Incorrect: Did you were tired?

Correct: Were you tired?

## 10. Comparing Past and Present Life

We can use both tenses to describe changes in our lives.

Examples:

- I lived in Boston in 2020, but I live in Chicago now.
- She worked in a restaurant last year, but she works in a bank now.
- We played soccer every weekend when we were children, but we play basketball now.
- He studied French at school, but he studies English now.
- They lived in a small apartment before, but they live in a big house now.

These sentences describe a past situation and a present situation.

## 11. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using the past form after didn't

Incorrect: I didn't went to work.

Correct: I didn't go to work.

### Mistake 2: Using the past form after did

Incorrect: Did she called you?

Correct: Did she call you?

### Mistake 3: Forgetting -s in the present simple

Incorrect: He work every day.

Correct: He works every day.

### Mistake 4: Using did with the verb be

Incorrect: Did you were at home?

Correct: Were you at home?

### Mistake 5: Using present simple with a finished past time

Incorrect: I visit my friend yesterday.

Correct: I visited my friend yesterday.

### Mistake 6: Using past simple for a current routine

Incorrect: She went to work every day. (when describing her current routine)

Correct: She goes to work every day.

## 12. American English Notes

Both the present simple and past simple are essential in everyday American English.

Common American English examples:

- I got home late last night.
- We went out for dinner yesterday.
- She usually gets coffee before work.
- He works downtown.
- Did you see that movie?
- I didn't see him yesterday.

In everyday American English, **got** is the past form of **get**. It is commonly used in expressions such as "I got home late."

Remember that **last night**, **yesterday**, and **ago** normally indicate a completed past time.

## 15. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- The present simple describes routines, habits, and facts.
- The past simple describes completed actions in the past.
- Present simple uses do/does in questions and don't/doesn't in negative sentences.
- Past simple uses did in questions and didn't in negative sentences.
- Use the base form of the verb after do, does, did, don't, doesn't, and didn't.
- The past forms of be are was and were.
- Time expressions help identify when an action happens.
