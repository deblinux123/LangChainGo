# Chapter 2: Verb To Be (Am, Is, Are)

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, students will be able to:

- Understand the verb to be.
- Use am, is, and are correctly.
- Make positive and negative sentences.
- Ask and answer Yes/No questions.
- Ask questions using WH-words.
- Use contractions in everyday English.
- Describe people, places, and situations.

## 2. What Is the Verb To Be?

The verb "to be" is one of the most important verbs in English.

We use it to talk about:

- Names and identities.
- Jobs and professions.
- Feelings and emotions.
- Ages.
- Locations.
- Nationalities.
- Descriptions.

Examples:

- I am Farid.
- She is a teacher.
- They are happy.
- He is 25 years old.
- We are at home.
- I am from Iran.

## 3. Forms of the Verb To Be

The present forms of the verb to be are am, is, and are.

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | I am a student. |
| You | are | You are friendly. |
| He | is | He is a doctor. |
| She | is | She is happy. |
| It | is | It is cold. |
| We | are | We are ready. |
| They | are | They are students. |

### Important Rule

- Use am with I.
- Use is with he, she, and it.
- Use are with you, we, and they.

## 4. Positive Sentences

### Structure

Subject + am/is/are + Complement

Examples:

- I am a student.
- You are my friend.
- He is a programmer.
- She is from New York.
- It is a beautiful day.
- We are ready.
- They are at school.

The complement can be a noun, adjective, or location.

### Examples by Category

Identity:
- I am Farid.
- She is my sister.

Profession:
- He is a software engineer.
- They are teachers.

Feelings:
- I am tired.
- She is excited.
- We are happy.

Location:
- The keys are on the table.
- My brother is at work.

Age:
- I am 25 years old.
- My parents are 50 years old.

## 5. Negative Sentences

To make a negative sentence, add not after am, is, or are.

### Structure

Subject + am/is/are + not + Complement

Examples:

- I am not tired.
- He is not a teacher.
- She is not at home.
- We are not busy.
- They are not ready.

### Contractions

| Full Form | Short Form |
|---|---|
| is not | isn't |
| are not | aren't |

Examples:

- He isn't tired.
- She isn't a doctor.
- We aren't late.
- They aren't students.

With I am not, the usual conversational form is "I'm not."

Examples:

- I'm not hungry.
- I'm not from Canada.

## 6. Questions with the Verb To Be

To make a question, move am, is, or are before the subject.

### Structure

Am/Is/Are + Subject + Complement?

Examples:

- Am I late?
- Are you a student?
- Is he your brother?
- Is she at home?
- Are they ready?

### Short Answers

| Question | Positive Answer | Negative Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Are you tired? | Yes, I am. | No, I'm not. |
| Is he a teacher? | Yes, he is. | No, he isn't. |
| Is she happy? | Yes, she is. | No, she isn't. |
| Are they ready? | Yes, they are. | No, they aren't. |

Important:

Do not use do or does with the verb to be when making these questions.

Correct:
- Are you happy?

Incorrect:
- Do you are happy?

## 7. WH-Questions with the Verb To Be

WH-words help us ask for specific information.

| WH-Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| What | Information or things |
| Where | Places |
| Who | People |
| When | Time |
| Why | Reasons |
| How | Manner or condition |

### Structure

WH-Word + am/is/are + Subject?

Examples:

- What is your name?
- Where are you from?
- Who is your teacher?
- When is your birthday?
- Why are you sad?
- How are you?

### Example Conversation

A: What is your name?
B: My name is Alex.

A: Where are you from?
B: I'm from Chicago.

A: How are you?
B: I'm great, thanks!

## 8. Contractions in Everyday American English

Contractions are shortened forms commonly used in spoken and informal written English.

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| I am | I'm |
| You are | You're |
| He is | He's |
| She is | She's |
| It is | It's |
| We are | We're |
| They are | They're |

Examples:

- I'm a student.
- You're very kind.
- He's my brother.
- She's at work.
- It's a nice day.
- We're ready.
- They're from Texas.

Use contractions naturally in everyday conversations. In formal writing, full forms may be preferred depending on context.

## 9. Common Mistakes

Incorrect: I is a student.

Correct: I am a student.

Incorrect: She are happy.

Correct: She is happy.

Incorrect: They is at home.

Correct: They are at home.

Incorrect: He not a doctor.

Correct: He is not a doctor.

Incorrect: Do you are tired?

Correct: Are you tired?

Incorrect: I have 25 years old.

Correct: I am 25 years old.

## 10. American English Notes

This chapter uses standard American English.

The verb to be follows the same basic present-tense rules in American and British English.

Common American conversational forms include:

- I'm ready.
- You're right.
- He's busy.
- We're late.

The contractions are also common in British English.

## 13. Chapter Summary

Remember these basic rules:

- I + am
- He/She/It + is
- You/We/They + are
- Add not to make negative sentences.
- Move am, is, or are before the subject to make questions.
- Do not use do or does with the verb to be in these sentence patterns.
