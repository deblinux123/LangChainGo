# Chapter 3: Subject and Object Pronouns

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, students will be able to:

- Understand subject pronouns.
- Understand object pronouns.
- Identify the subject and object in a sentence.
- Choose the correct pronoun.
- Avoid common pronoun mistakes.
- Use pronouns in everyday American English.

## 2. What Are Pronouns?

A pronoun is a word that replaces a noun.

We use pronouns to avoid repeating names, people, animals, or things.

Examples:

Without pronouns:
- Sarah is a teacher. Sarah works at a school.

With pronouns:
- Sarah is a teacher. She works at a school.

Without pronouns:
- I have a new phone. My phone is expensive.

With pronouns:
- I have a new phone. It is expensive.

There are different types of pronouns. In this chapter, we focus on subject and object pronouns.

## 3. Subject Pronouns

Subject pronouns replace the person or thing that performs an action or is described in a sentence.

### Subject Pronoun Table

| Subject Pronoun | Meaning |
|---|---|
| I | The speaker |
| You | The person or people being spoken to |
| He | A male person |
| She | A female person |
| It | An animal, object, or thing |
| We | The speaker and other people |
| They | Other people, animals, or things |

### Sentence Structure

Subject Pronoun + Verb + Complement

Examples:

- I study English.
- You speak clearly.
- He works in an office.
- She likes music.
- It looks beautiful.
- We live in Chicago.
- They play soccer.

### Subject Pronouns with the Verb To Be

| Subject | Verb To Be | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | I am happy. |
| You | are | You are kind. |
| He | is | He is a student. |
| She | is | She is a doctor. |
| It | is | It is cold. |
| We | are | We are ready. |
| They | are | They are friends. |

## 4. Object Pronouns

Object pronouns receive the action of a verb or follow a preposition.

### Object Pronoun Table

| Subject Pronoun | Object Pronoun |
|---|---|
| I | me |
| You | you |
| He | him |
| She | her |
| It | it |
| We | us |
| They | them |

### Examples

- My friend called me.
- I can help you.
- We know him.
- I like her.
- Please open it.
- The teacher helped us.
- I invited them.

## 5. Subject vs. Object Pronouns

The main difference is their role in a sentence.

A subject pronoun performs the action.

An object pronoun receives the action.

### Compare the Examples

| Subject Pronoun | Object Pronoun |
|---|---|
| I love her. | She loves me. |
| He knows us. | We know him. |
| They help me. | I help them. |
| We invited him. | He invited us. |

### Example 1

Sarah likes John.

- Sarah is the subject.
- John is the object.

Using pronouns:

She likes him.

### Example 2

Tom called Mary.

- Tom is the subject.
- Mary is the object.

Using pronouns:

He called her.

## 6. Object Pronouns After Prepositions

Use object pronouns after prepositions such as:

- to
- for
- with
- about
- from
- at

Examples:

- This gift is for you.
- I live with him.
- She is talking to me.
- They are waiting for us.
- I heard about her.
- This letter is from them.

Incorrect:
- She is talking to I.

Correct:
- She is talking to me.

## 7. Pronouns in Everyday Conversations

### Conversation 1: Introducing Friends

Alex: Who is that person?

Emma: He is my brother.

Alex: Do you live with him?

Emma: Yes, I live with him.

### Conversation 2: At School

Teacher: Do you know Sarah?

Student: Yes, I know her.

Teacher: Does she know you?

Student: Yes, she knows me.

### Conversation 3: Helping a Friend

Mike: Can you help me?

John: Of course! I can help you.

Mike: Thank you. You're a great friend.

## 8. Common Mistakes

Incorrect: Me am a student.

Correct: I am a student.

Incorrect: Her is my sister.

Correct: She is my sister.

Incorrect: Him likes soccer.

Correct: He likes soccer.

Incorrect: She called I.

Correct: She called me.

Incorrect: They invited we.

Correct: They invited us.

Incorrect: This gift is for she.

Correct: This gift is for her.

## 9. American English Notes

Subject and object pronouns are used in the same basic way in standard American and British English.

In everyday American English, contractions are common:

- He's my friend.
- She's my teacher.
- They're waiting for us.
- I'm talking to him.

Remember that "you" can refer to one person or multiple people.

In casual American speech, people sometimes say "you guys" when addressing a group. This is an informal expression, not a replacement for the standard pronoun "you" in formal writing.

## 12. Chapter Summary

Remember:

- Subject pronouns perform the action or are the subject of a sentence.
- Object pronouns receive the action or follow prepositions.

Subject pronouns:

I, you, he, she, it, we, they.

Object pronouns:

me, you, him, her, it, us, them.

Examples:

- She knows me.
- I know her.
- They help us.
- We help them.
