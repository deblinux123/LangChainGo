# Chapter 6: Singular and Plural Nouns

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, students will be able to:

- Understand singular and plural nouns.
- Form regular plural nouns.
- Use -s and -es correctly.
- Understand spelling changes with -y and -f/-fe.
- Recognize common irregular plural nouns.
- Use singular and plural nouns in sentences.
- Avoid common pluralization mistakes.

## 2. What Are Singular and Plural Nouns?

A singular noun refers to one person, animal, place, or thing.

Examples:
- one book
- one student
- one dog
- one city

A plural noun refers to two or more people, animals, places, or things.

Examples:
- two books
- three students
- four dogs
- many cities

Examples in sentences:

- I have one brother.
- She has two brothers.
- There is one car outside.
- There are three cars outside.

## 3. Regular Plural Nouns: Add -S

For most nouns, add -s to make the plural.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| book | books |
| car | cars |
| dog | dogs |
| student | students |
| phone | phones |
| computer | computers |
| table | tables |
| chair | chairs |

Examples:

- I have two books.
- Three students are in the classroom.
- They own two cars.

## 4. Add -ES

Add -es to nouns ending in:

- -s
- -ss
- -sh
- -ch
- -x
- -z

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| bus | buses |
| class | classes |
| dish | dishes |
| watch | watches |
| box | boxes |
| quiz | quizzes |

Examples:

- There are three buses.
- I washed the dishes.
- She has two watches.

Note: Some words ending in -o also take -es, such as tomatoes and potatoes. Others take only -s, such as photos and pianos. Learn common forms individually.

## 5. Nouns Ending in -Y

### A. Consonant + Y

Change -y to -ies.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| city | cities |
| baby | babies |
| story | stories |
| family | families |
| country | countries |

Examples:

- There are many cities in the United States.
- The families live nearby.

### B. Vowel + Y

Simply add -s.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| boy | boys |
| toy | toys |
| key | keys |
| day | days |
| monkey | monkeys |

Examples:

- The boys are playing.
- I have two keys.

## 6. Nouns Ending in -F or -FE

Some nouns change -f or -fe to -ves.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| leaf | leaves |
| wife | wives |
| knife | knives |
| life | lives |
| wolf | wolves |

However, some nouns simply take -s.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| roof | roofs |
| belief | beliefs |
| chef | chefs |
| cliff | cliffs |

Important: These are spelling patterns, not universal rules. Some nouns must be learned individually.

## 7. Irregular Plural Nouns

Irregular nouns do not follow the usual plural spelling rules.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| man | men |
| woman | women |
| child | children |
| person | people |
| tooth | teeth |
| foot | feet |
| mouse | mice |
| goose | geese |

Examples:

- There are three children in the park.
- The women are teachers.
- My feet are cold.
- There are many people at the concert.
- The mice are small.

## 8. Nouns That Stay the Same

Some nouns have the same singular and plural forms.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| sheep | sheep |
| deer | deer |
| fish | fish |
| species | species |

Examples:

- I see one sheep.
- I see five sheep.
- There is one deer.
- There are two deer.

Note: "Fishes" can be used when referring to different species of fish, but "fish" is the usual plural in everyday contexts.

## 9. Singular and Plural Verb Agreement

A singular subject usually takes a singular verb form.

A plural subject usually takes a plural verb form.

Examples:

- The student is happy.
- The students are happy.
- The dog runs fast.
- The dogs run fast.

With the present simple:

- He works.
- They work.
- She likes coffee.
- We like coffee.

Remember:

Do not add -s to the main verb with plural subjects such as they, we, and you.

## 10. Using A and An with Nouns

Use a or an with singular countable nouns.

Examples:

- a book
- a student
- an apple
- an egg

Do not use a or an directly before plural nouns.

Correct:
- I have a book.
- I have two books.

Incorrect:
- I have a books.

## 11. Common Mistakes

Incorrect: two childs

Correct: two children

Incorrect: three citys

Correct: three cities

Incorrect: two boxs

Correct: two boxes

Incorrect: five mans

Correct: five men

Incorrect: two sheeps

Correct: two sheep

Incorrect: The students is happy.

Correct: The students are happy.

## 12. American English Notes

The pluralization rules in this chapter are generally the same in American and British English.

Examples commonly used in American English:

- apartments
- stores
- neighborhoods
- cookies
- trucks

The spelling of regular plurals does not normally change between the two varieties.

## 15. Chapter Summary

Remember:

- Most nouns take -s.
- Nouns ending in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, and -z often take -es.
- Consonant + y changes to -ies.
- Vowel + y takes -s.
- Some -f and -fe nouns change to -ves.
- Irregular nouns have special plural forms.
- Some nouns have the same singular and plural form.
- Singular and plural nouns must agree with their verbs.
