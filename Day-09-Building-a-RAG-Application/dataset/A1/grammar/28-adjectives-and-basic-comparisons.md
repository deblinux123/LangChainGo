# Chapter 28 — Adjectives and Basic Comparisons

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand what adjectives are.
- Describe people, places, animals, and things.
- Use adjectives before nouns and after linking verbs.
- Compare two people or things using comparative adjectives.
- Use **than** in comparisons.
- Recognize common adjective spelling changes.

## 2. What Are Adjectives?

An adjective is a word that describes a noun or pronoun.

Adjectives tell us more about people, places, animals, and things.

Examples:

- a big house
- a small dog
- a beautiful city
- a happy child
- an interesting book
- a cold drink

### Common Adjectives

| Adjective | Opposite |
|---|---|
| Big | Small |
| Tall | Short |
| Long | Short |
| Fast | Slow |
| Hot | Cold |
| Old | New / Young |
| Happy | Sad |
| Easy | Difficult |
| Beautiful | Ugly |
| Expensive | Cheap |
| Clean | Dirty |
| Strong | Weak |
| Friendly | Unfriendly |
| Interesting | Boring |

Examples:

- My house is big.
- The movie is interesting.
- She is friendly.
- This coffee is hot.
- That car is expensive.

## 3. Adjectives Before Nouns

In English, adjectives usually come before the nouns they describe.

**Structure:**

Adjective + Noun

Examples:

- a beautiful garden
- a small apartment
- an expensive car
- a friendly teacher
- a new computer
- an old building

More examples:

| Adjective | Noun | Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Big | House | A big house |
| Small | Dog | A small dog |
| Beautiful | Flower | A beautiful flower |
| Fast | Car | A fast car |
| Interesting | Book | An interesting book |

Important: Adjectives do not usually change to agree with singular or plural nouns.

Examples:

- a small dog
- small dogs
- a beautiful flower
- beautiful flowers

Incorrect: smalls dogs

Correct: small dogs

## 4. Adjectives After the Verb "Be"

We can also use adjectives after the verb **be** (am, is, are).

**Structure:**

Subject + am / is / are + Adjective

Examples:

- I am happy.
- She is beautiful.
- He is tall.
- They are friendly.
- The weather is cold.
- These books are interesting.

Compare:

- She is a happy person.
- She is happy.

Both sentences are correct.

## 5. Adjectives After Other Linking Verbs

Some common verbs can connect a subject to an adjective.

At A1 level, learn these examples:

- look
- feel
- smell
- taste
- sound

Examples:

- You look tired.
- I feel happy.
- The flowers smell nice.
- This soup tastes delicious.
- That sounds interesting.

These verbs describe how someone or something appears, feels, smells, tastes, or sounds.

## 6. What Are Comparative Adjectives?

We use comparative adjectives to compare two people, places, animals, or things.

Examples:

- John is taller than Mike.
- This car is faster than that car.
- My house is bigger than your house.
- Today is colder than yesterday.
- This book is more interesting than that book.

We usually use **than** after a comparative adjective when mentioning the second person or thing.

## 7. Comparative Adjectives: Short Adjectives

For many short adjectives, add **-er**.

**Structure:**

Subject + be + adjective-er + than + noun / pronoun

| Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| Tall | Taller |
| Short | Shorter |
| Fast | Faster |
| Slow | Slower |
| Small | Smaller |
| Long | Longer |
| Old | Older |
| Young | Younger |
| Cheap | Cheaper |
| Cold | Colder |

Examples:

- My brother is taller than me.
- A car is faster than a bicycle.
- This bag is smaller than that bag.
- Winter is colder than summer.
- My new phone is cheaper than my old phone.

## 8. Spelling Rules for Comparative Adjectives

### Rule 1: Most Short Adjectives

Add **-er**.

| Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| Fast | Faster |
| Tall | Taller |
| Small | Smaller |
| Cheap | Cheaper |

### Rule 2: Adjectives Ending in -e

Add **-r**.

| Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| Nice | Nicer |
| Large | Larger |
| Wide | Wider |
| Safe | Safer |

### Rule 3: Consonant + Vowel + Consonant

For many short adjectives with this pattern, double the final consonant and add **-er**.

| Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| Big | Bigger |
| Hot | Hotter |
| Thin | Thinner |
| Fat | Fatter |

### Rule 4: Adjectives Ending in Consonant + y

Change **y** to **i** and add **-er**.

| Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| Happy | Happier |
| Easy | Easier |
| Busy | Busier |
| Heavy | Heavier |

## 9. Comparative Adjectives: Long Adjectives

For many adjectives with two or more syllables, use **more** before the adjective.

**Structure:**

More + adjective + than

Examples:

- more beautiful than
- more expensive than
- more interesting than
- more comfortable than
- more difficult than

Sentences:

- This city is more beautiful than that city.
- My new laptop is more expensive than my old laptop.
- This movie is more interesting than the last one.
- English is more difficult than I expected.
- This chair is more comfortable than that chair.

Do not normally add **-er** to these adjectives.

Incorrect: beautifuler

Correct: more beautiful

Incorrect: expensiver

Correct: more expensive

## 10. Irregular Comparative Adjectives

Some adjectives have special comparative forms.

| Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| Good | Better |
| Bad | Worse |
| Far | Farther / Further |
| Little | Less |
| Much / Many | More |

Examples:

- This restaurant is better than that one.
- My headache is worse today.
- My school is farther from home than yours.
- I have less free time than my brother.
- She has more books than me.

At A1 level, focus especially on **good → better** and **bad → worse**.

## 11. Comparing with "As ... As"

We use **as + adjective + as** to say that two things are equal in a particular way.

**Structure:**

As + adjective + as

Examples:

- My brother is as tall as me.
- This book is as interesting as that one.
- The blue car is as fast as the red car.
- Today is as cold as yesterday.

For negative comparisons, use **not as ... as**.

Examples:

- This bag is not as expensive as that bag.
- He is not as tall as his brother.
- This movie is not as interesting as the first one.

## 12. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using "more" with a short adjective

Incorrect: more taller

Correct: taller

### Mistake 2: Forgetting "than"

Incorrect: My car is faster your car.

Correct: My car is faster than your car.

### Mistake 3: Using -er with long adjectives

Incorrect: beautifuler

Correct: more beautiful

### Mistake 4: Forgetting the spelling change

Incorrect: happyer

Correct: happier

### Mistake 5: Changing the adjective for plural nouns

Incorrect: These are beautifuls flowers.

Correct: These are beautiful flowers.

### Mistake 6: Using the wrong irregular form

Incorrect: This book is gooder than that one.

Correct: This book is better than that one.

## 13. American English Notes

These adjective and comparison forms are common in American English.

Examples:

- This apartment is cheaper than my old one.
- That restaurant is better than the one downtown.
- My brother is taller than me.
- This movie is more interesting.
- The weather is colder today.

In everyday American English, it is common to hear:

- "It's way bigger."
- "This one is much cheaper."
- "That movie is a lot better."

Words such as **much**, **a lot**, and **way** can make comparisons stronger.

## 16. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- Adjectives describe nouns and pronouns.
- Adjectives usually come before nouns.
- Adjectives can also come after linking verbs such as be, look, and feel.
- Use **-er** with many short adjectives to make comparisons.
- Use **more** with many longer adjectives.
- Use **than** to introduce the second part of a comparison.
- Some adjectives have irregular comparative forms, such as good → better.
- Use **as ... as** to compare equal qualities.
- Use **not as ... as** to compare unequal qualities.

### Quick Review

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| Adjective + noun | A beautiful house |
| Be + adjective | The house is beautiful. |
| Short comparative | Taller than |
| Long comparative | More expensive than |
| Irregular comparative | Better than |
| Equal comparison | As tall as |
| Unequal comparison | Not as tall as |
