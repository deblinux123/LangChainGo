# Chapter 20: Future with Will

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Use **will** to talk about the future.
- Make positive and negative sentences.
- Ask yes/no and Wh-questions.
- Make predictions and express spontaneous decisions.
- Make promises, offers, and requests.

## 2. What Is Will?

We use **will** to talk about future events, predictions, decisions, promises, and offers.

Examples:

- I will call you tomorrow.
- She will be here soon.
- They will travel next year.
- I think it will rain tomorrow.

The word **will** is a modal auxiliary verb. It does not change according to the subject.

## 3. Positive Sentences

### Structure

**Subject + will + base verb + complement**

| Subject | Example |
|---|---|
| I | I will study tonight. |
| You | You will enjoy the movie. |
| He | He will call you. |
| She | She will arrive tomorrow. |
| It | It will rain soon. |
| We | We will help you. |
| They | They will visit us. |

Important: Always use the base form of the main verb after will.

Correct:
- She will work.
- They will go.
- He will study.

Incorrect:
- She will works.
- They will went.
- He will studied.

## 4. Contractions

In everyday English, **will** is often shortened to **'ll**.

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| I will | I'll |
| You will | You'll |
| He will | He'll |
| She will | She'll |
| It will | It'll |
| We will | We'll |
| They will | They'll |

Examples:

- I'll see you tomorrow.
- She'll call later.
- We'll help you.
- They'll arrive soon.

## 5. Negative Sentences

Use **will not** or **won't**.

### Structure

**Subject + will not + base verb + complement**

Examples:

- I will not go tomorrow.
- She will not work tonight.
- They will not come to the party.
- We will not forget you.

### Contraction

**Will not = Won't**

Examples:

- I won't go tomorrow.
- He won't call tonight.
- We won't be late.
- They won't buy a new car.

## 6. Yes/No Questions

Put **will** before the subject.

### Structure

**Will + subject + base verb + complement?**

Examples:

- Will you help me?
- Will she come tomorrow?
- Will they travel next year?
- Will he call you?

### Short Answers

| Question | Positive Answer | Negative Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Will you help me? | Yes, I will. | No, I won't. |
| Will she come? | Yes, she will. | No, she won't. |
| Will they travel? | Yes, they will. | No, they won't. |
| Will he call? | Yes, he will. | No, he won't. |

## 7. Wh- Questions

Put a question word before **will**.

### Structure

**Question word + will + subject + base verb?**

Examples:

| Question Word | Example |
|---|---|
| What | What will you do tomorrow? |
| Where | Where will they stay? |
| When | When will she arrive? |
| Why | Why will he leave? |
| Who | Who will you invite? |
| How | How will we get there? |

More examples:

- What will you study?
- Where will you work?
- When will they call?
- How will she travel?

## 8. Uses of Will

### Use 1: Predictions

Use will to make predictions about the future.

Examples:

- I think it will rain tomorrow.
- She will be a great teacher.
- People will use more technology in the future.
- Our team will win the game.

Common expressions:

- I think...
- I believe...
- I hope...
- Probably...
- Maybe...

Examples:

- I think he will come.
- Maybe they will visit us.
- She will probably pass the exam.

### Use 2: Spontaneous Decisions

Use will when you decide to do something at the moment of speaking.

Examples:

- The phone is ringing. I'll answer it.
- I'm thirsty. I'll get some water.
- It's cold. I'll close the window.
- You look tired. I'll help you.

### Use 3: Promises

Use will to make promises.

Examples:

- I will help you.
- I won't tell anyone.
- I will call you tonight.
- We will never forget this day.

### Use 4: Offers

Use will to offer help or assistance.

Examples:

- I'll carry your bag.
- I'll open the door.
- I'll help you with your homework.
- We'll give you a ride.

### Use 5: Requests

Use **Will you...?** to make a request.

Examples:

- Will you help me?
- Will you close the door?
- Will you wait a minute?
- Will you call me later?

These questions are common in everyday English. Depending on the situation, they can sound direct; **Could you...?** is another polite option.

## 9. Future-Time Expressions

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| tomorrow | I will call you tomorrow. |
| tonight | She will study tonight. |
| soon | They will arrive soon. |
| later | I'll see you later. |
| next week | We will travel next week. |
| next month | He will start next month. |
| next year | They will move next year. |
| in the future | Technology will change in the future. |
| one day | One day, I will visit Japan. |

## 10. Will vs. Going To

Both forms can talk about the future, but they are often used differently.

| Will | Going To |
|---|---|
| Often used for spontaneous decisions | Often used for existing plans |
| Often used for predictions and opinions | Often used for predictions based on present evidence |
| I'll answer the phone. | I'm going to visit my friend tomorrow. |
| I think it will rain. | Look at those clouds! It's going to rain. |

Examples:

- "I'm going to study tonight." — I already have this plan.
- "I'll study tonight." — I am deciding or offering now.

These are common tendencies, not absolute rules. Both forms can sometimes express predictions or intentions.

## 11. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| She will goes. | She will go. |
| He will studies. | He will study. |
| They will went. | They will go. |
| I will to call you. | I will call you. |
| She wills come. | She will come. |
| Will you helps me? | Will you help me? |
| I don't will go. | I won't go. |

## 12. American English Notes

These examples use standard American English.

- "I'll" and "won't" are very common in everyday conversation.
- "I'll get it" is a natural response when someone knocks or the phone rings.
- "I'll give you a ride" means offering to take someone somewhere in a vehicle.
- "See you later" is a common informal expression.

Examples:

- I'll grab some coffee.
- I'll give you a ride.
- We'll see you tomorrow.
- Don't worry. I'll help you.

## 15. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- Use **will + base verb** for future statements.
- Use **won't + base verb** for negative sentences.
- Put **will** before the subject to ask questions.
- Use will for predictions, spontaneous decisions, promises, offers, and requests.
- Use future-time expressions such as tomorrow, soon, and next week.
- Understand the common difference between will and going to.

### Key Structures

| Sentence Type | Structure |
|---|---|
| Positive | Subject + will + base verb |
| Negative | Subject + won't + base verb |
| Yes/No Question | Will + subject + base verb? |
| Wh-Question | Question word + will + subject + base verb? |
