# Chapter 37 — Basic Contractions and Informal English

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand contractions in English.
- Use contractions with the verb "to be."
- Use contractions with "do not," "does not," and "did not."
- Use contractions with modal verbs.
- Recognize common informal expressions.
- Understand the difference between formal and informal English.

## 2. What Are Contractions?

A contraction is a shorter form of two words.

We use an apostrophe (`'`) to show that some letters are missing.

Examples:

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| I am | I'm |
| You are | You're |
| He is | He's |
| She is | She's |
| It is | It's |
| We are | We're |
| They are | They're |

Examples in sentences:

- I am a student. → I'm a student.
- She is happy. → She's happy.
- They are at home. → They're at home.
- We are ready. → We're ready.

Contractions are very common in everyday spoken English.

## 3. Contractions with the Verb "To Be"

The verb **to be** has common contracted forms.

### Positive Contractions

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| I am | I'm |
| You are | You're |
| He is | He's |
| She is | She's |
| It is | It's |
| We are | We're |
| They are | They're |

Examples:

- I'm from the United States.
- You're my friend.
- He's a teacher.
- She's at work.
- It's cold today.
- We're students.
- They're happy.

### Negative Contractions

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| I am not | I'm not |
| You are not | You aren't |
| He is not | He isn't |
| She is not | She isn't |
| It is not | It isn't |
| We are not | We aren't |
| They are not | They aren't |

Examples:

- I'm not tired.
- You aren't late.
- He isn't at home.
- She isn't angry.
- It isn't expensive.
- We aren't busy.
- They aren't ready.

**Important:**

The usual negative contraction of **I am not** is **I'm not**.

Example:

- I'm not hungry.

In questions, the common tag form is:

- I'm late, aren't I?

## 4. Contractions with HAVE

The verb **have** can also form contractions when it is an auxiliary verb.

Examples:

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| I have | I've |
| You have | You've |
| He has | He's |
| She has | She's |
| We have | We've |
| They have | They've |

Examples:

- I've finished my homework.
- You've done a good job.
- He's already eaten.
- She's visited New York.
- We've finished the project.
- They've arrived.

**Important:**

Some contractions have more than one possible meaning.

For example:

- He's = He is or He has.
- She's = She is or She has.
- It's = It is or It has.

The sentence context tells us the meaning.

Examples:

- He's happy. (= He is happy.)
- He's finished. (= He has finished.)

At this level, focus on recognizing the meaning from the sentence.

## 5. Negative Contractions with DO

Use contractions with **do not**, **does not**, and **did not**.

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| Do not | Don't |
| Does not | Doesn't |
| Did not | Didn't |

Examples:

- I don't like coffee.
- You don't work here.
- He doesn't play soccer.
- She doesn't drive.
- We didn't watch TV.
- They didn't go to school.

Remember:

- Use **don't** with I, you, we, and they.
- Use **doesn't** with he, she, and it.
- Use **didn't** for all subjects in the past simple.

After don't, doesn't, and didn't, use the base form of the verb.

Incorrect: She doesn't likes coffee.

Correct: She doesn't like coffee.

Incorrect: He didn't went home.

Correct: He didn't go home.

## 6. Contractions with WILL

Use **'ll** for will and **won't** for will not.

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| I will | I'll |
| You will | You'll |
| He will | He'll |
| She will | She'll |
| It will | It'll |
| We will | We'll |
| They will | They'll |
| Will not | Won't |

Examples:

- I'll call you tomorrow.
- You'll enjoy the movie.
- He'll be here soon.
- She'll help us.
- We'll visit our friends.
- They'll come later.
- I won't forget.

## 7. Contractions with Other Modal Verbs

Some modal verbs also have contracted forms.

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| Cannot | Can't |
| Could not | Couldn't |
| Should not | Shouldn't |
| Would not | Wouldn't |
| Must not | Mustn't |

Examples:

- I can't swim.
- She couldn't come yesterday.
- You shouldn't eat too much candy.
- He wouldn't tell me.
- You mustn't park here.

Remember:

After a modal verb, use the base form of the main verb.

Correct: She can't drive.

Incorrect: She can't drives.

## 8. Contractions with WOULD

The contraction **'d** can mean **would** or **had**, depending on the sentence.

Examples:

- I'd like some coffee. (= I would like some coffee.)
- She'd help you. (= She would help you.)
- They'd finished the work. (= They had finished the work.)

For beginner English, focus on these common expressions:

- I'd like a coffee.
- I'd like some water.
- We'd like to order.
- He'd like to go home.

## 9. Informal English in Everyday Conversations

Informal English is common when speaking with friends, family, and people you know well.

Examples:

| More Formal | Common Informal Form |
|---|---|
| I am | I'm |
| We are | We're |
| Do not | Don't |
| Cannot | Can't |
| I will | I'll |
| I would | I'd |

Examples:

**Conversation 1**

A: How are you?

B: I'm good, thanks. How about you?

**Conversation 2**

A: Do you like coffee?

B: No, I don't. I prefer tea.

**Conversation 3**

A: Are you coming tomorrow?

B: Yes, I'll be there.

**Conversation 4**

A: Can you help me?

B: Sure, I can.

## 10. Common Informal Expressions

Here are some useful expressions in American English.

| Expression | Meaning |
|---|---|
| What's up? | How are you? / What's happening? |
| Gonna | Going to |
| Wanna | Want to |
| Gotta | Have got to / Have to |
| Kinda | Kind of |
| Yep | Yes |
| Nope | No |
| Hang out | Spend time socially |

Examples:

- What's up?
- I'm gonna call you.
- I wanna learn English.
- I gotta go now.
- It's kinda cold today.
- Yep, I'm ready.
- Nope, I don't know.
- Let's hang out this weekend.

**Important:**

Forms such as **gonna**, **wanna**, and **gotta** are informal spoken forms. In standard formal writing, use:

- going to
- want to
- have to

## 11. Formal vs. Informal English

Different situations require different levels of formality.

### Formal Situations

Use full forms in formal writing, academic work, and professional communication when appropriate.

Examples:

- I am writing to request information.
- We do not have the required documents.
- She cannot attend the meeting.

### Informal Situations

Contractions are natural in everyday conversations and many informal messages.

Examples:

- I'm writing to ask a question.
- We don't have the documents.
- She can't come to the meeting.

**Note:** Contractions are also acceptable in many forms of professional and academic writing. The appropriate choice depends on the style and context.

## 12. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Confusing its and it's

- It's = It is or It has.
- Its = Possessive form.

Examples:

- It's a nice day.
- The dog is eating its food.

### Mistake 2: Confusing your and you're

- You're = You are.
- Your = Possessive form.

Examples:

- You're my friend.
- Your phone is new.

### Mistake 3: Using the wrong apostrophe

Incorrect: Dont worry.

Correct: Don't worry.

### Mistake 4: Using the wrong verb after a contraction

Incorrect: He doesn't likes music.

Correct: He doesn't like music.

### Mistake 5: Confusing he's and his

- He's = He is or He has.
- His = Possessive form.

Examples:

- He's my brother.
- His car is blue.

### Mistake 6: Using informal spelling in formal writing

Informal: I wanna learn English.

Standard: I want to learn English.

## 13. American English Notes

Contractions are extremely common in spoken American English.

You will often hear:

- I'm gonna go.
- I don't know.
- She's gonna call you.
- We've got to leave.
- Wanna grab some coffee?
- What's up?

Remember:

- **Gonna** represents "going to" in informal speech.
- **Wanna** represents "want to" in informal speech.
- **Gotta** commonly represents "got to" or "have got to."

These forms are useful for understanding everyday conversation, movies, and informal videos.

For standard writing, use the complete forms.

## 16. Chapter Summary

- Contractions are shorter forms of two words.

- Informal expressions such as gonna and wanna are common in spoken American English.
