# Chapter 32 — Question Words: Who, What, Which, Whose, How

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand the meaning of common question words.
- Ask questions about people, things, choices, ownership, and manner.
- Use **who**, **what**, **which**, **whose**, and **how** correctly.
- Form questions with **do**, **does**, **is**, **are**, and **can**.
- Give short and complete answers to questions.

## 2. What Are Question Words?

Question words are words that we use to ask for specific information.

They usually appear at the beginning of a question.

Examples:

- Who is your teacher?
- What is your name?
- Which color do you prefer?
- Whose bag is this?
- How are you?

Different question words ask for different kinds of information.

## 3. WHO — Asking About People

Use **who** to ask about a person or people.

Examples:

- Who is your best friend?
- Who is that man?
- Who are your parents?
- Who is your English teacher?
- Who lives here?

### Common Question Patterns

**Who + be + subject/complement?**

- Who is she?
- Who are they?

**Who + do/does + subject + base verb?**

- Who do you know?
- Who does she visit?

When **who** is the subject of the sentence, we usually do not use do or does.

Examples:

- Who lives here?
- Who works with you?
- Who speaks English?

### Answers

- Who is your teacher?
  - My teacher is Mr. Brown.

- Who is that woman?
  - She is my aunt.

- Who do you visit?
  - I visit my grandmother.

## 4. WHAT — Asking About Things and Information

Use **what** to ask about things, activities, names, jobs, and general information.

Examples:

- What is your name?
- What is this?
- What do you do?
- What does she want?
- What time is it?
- What are you doing?

### Common Question Patterns

**What + be + subject?**

- What is your name?
- What are these?

**What + do/does + subject + base verb?**

- What do you like?
- What does he eat?

**What + can + subject + base verb?**

- What can you cook?
- What can she do?

### Answers

- What is your name?
  - My name is Alex.

- What do you like?
  - I like music.

- What does he do?
  - He is a doctor.

## 5. WHICH — Asking About Choices

Use **which** when choosing between a limited number of people or things.

Examples:

- Which color do you like, red or blue?
- Which book is yours?
- Which shirt do you want?
- Which bus goes downtown?
- Which one is your phone?

### Which + Noun

We can use **which** directly before a noun.

Examples:

- Which movie do you want to watch?
- Which restaurant do you prefer?
- Which subject do you like?
- Which shoes are yours?

### Which One

Use **which one** when the noun is already clear or does not need to be repeated.

Examples:

- Which one do you want?
- Which one is yours?
- Which one is bigger?

### Answers

- Which color do you prefer, red or green?
  - I prefer green.

- Which book is yours?
  - The blue one is mine.

## 6. WHAT vs. WHICH

Both words ask about things, but they are not always interchangeable.

Use **what** for general or open questions.

Use **which** when asking someone to choose from a known or limited group.

Compare:

- What is your favorite color?
- Which color do you want, red or blue?

More examples:

| What | Which |
|---|---|
| What is your favorite food? | Which pizza do you want? |
| What do you study? | Which class do you attend? |
| What is your favorite movie? | Which movie should we watch, A or B? |

## 7. WHOSE — Asking About Ownership

Use **whose** to ask who owns something.

Examples:

- Whose bag is this?
- Whose phone is that?
- Whose shoes are these?
- Whose books are on the table?
- Whose car is outside?

### Common Question Patterns

**Whose + noun + be + this/that/these/those?**

Examples:

- Whose jacket is this?
- Whose keys are those?
- Whose pencils are these?

### Answers

- Whose phone is this?
  - It is Sarah's phone.
  - It is hers.

- Whose shoes are these?
  - They are Tom's shoes.
  - They are his.

### Whose vs. Who's

These words have different meanings.

- **Whose** asks about ownership.
- **Who's** is a contraction of **who is** or **who has**.

Examples:

- Whose book is this?
- Who's your teacher? (= Who is your teacher?)
- Who's got my keys? (= Who has got my keys?)

Do not confuse them.

## 8. HOW — Asking About Condition, Manner, and Method

Use **how** to ask about the way something happens or someone's condition.

Examples:

- How are you?
- How do you go to work?
- How does she speak English?
- How do you spell your name?
- How can I help you?

### Common Answers

- How are you?
  - I'm fine, thanks.

- How do you go to school?
  - I go by bus.

- How does he speak English?
  - He speaks slowly.

- How do you spell your name?
  - A-L-E-X.

## 9. Common Expressions with HOW

Some expressions use **how** with another word.

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| How old | Asking about age | How old are you? |
| How many | Asking about a countable quantity | How many books do you have? |
| How much | Asking about an uncountable quantity or price | How much water do you need? |
| How often | Asking about frequency | How often do you exercise? |
| How long | Asking about duration or length | How long is the movie? |
| How far | Asking about distance | How far is the school? |

Examples:

- How old is your brother?
  - He is 20 years old.

- How many students are in your class?
  - There are 15 students.

- How much is this shirt?
  - It is $25.

- How often do you study English?
  - Every day.

- How long is the movie?
  - It is two hours long.

- How far is the airport?
  - It is five miles away.

## 10. Question Word + Auxiliary Verb

Many questions follow this structure:

**Question Word + Auxiliary + Subject + Main Verb?**

Examples:

- What do you want?
- Where does she live?
- Which book do they like?
- How do you cook rice?

Remember:

- Use **do** with I, you, we, and they.
- Use **does** with he, she, and it.
- After do or does, use the base form of the main verb.

Incorrect: What does she likes?

Correct: What does she like?

## 11. Questions with the Verb BE

When the main verb is **be**, put it after the question word.

**Question Word + am/is/are + Subject?**

Examples:

- Who is your friend?
- What is your job?
- Which one is yours?
- Whose books are these?
- How are your parents?

Do not add do or does to questions with be.

Incorrect: What do you are?

Correct: What are you?

## 12. Questions with CAN

Use **can** to ask about ability or possibility.

**Question Word + can + Subject + Base Verb?**

Examples:

- What can you cook?
- Which languages can she speak?
- How can I help you?
- Who can drive this car?

Remember to use the base form of the verb after can.

Incorrect: What can she cooks?

Correct: What can she cook?

## 13. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Confusing what and which

Incorrect: Which is your favorite color? (When asking an open question about a favorite color.)

Better: What is your favorite color?

### Mistake 2: Using does with the base verb incorrectly

Incorrect: What does he wants?

Correct: What does he want?

### Mistake 3: Confusing whose and who's

Incorrect: Who's bag is this?

Correct: Whose bag is this?

### Mistake 4: Adding do or does with be

Incorrect: Where does she is?

Correct: Where is she?

### Mistake 5: Using the wrong question word

Incorrect: How is your name?

Correct: What is your name?

### Mistake 6: Forgetting the auxiliary

Incorrect: What you want?

Correct: What do you want?

## 14. American English Notes

These question words and patterns are standard in American English.

Common everyday questions include:

- "What's your name?"
- "Who's that?"
- "Which one do you want?"
- "Whose phone is this?"
- "How's it going?"

In informal American English:

- **What's** = What is
- **Who's** = Who is
- **How's** = How is

Examples:

- What's your favorite movie?
- Who's your new teacher?
- How's your day?

Remember that contractions are common in conversation, but use the full forms when you need a more formal style.

## 17. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- **Who** asks about people.
- **What** asks about things and general information.
- **Which** asks someone to choose from a limited group.
- **Whose** asks about ownership.
- **How** asks about condition, manner, or method.
- Use **do** and **does** for many present simple questions.
- Use **am**, **is**, and **are** when the main verb is be.
- Use **can** before the subject's base-form verb in ability questions.
- Expressions such as **how old**, **how many**, and **how often** ask for specific information.
