# Chapter 38: Basic Sentence Structure and Word Order

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand basic English sentence structure.
- Put subjects, verbs, and objects in the correct order.
- Build sentences with the verb "to be."
- Use adjectives in the correct position.
- Place adverbs and time expressions correctly.
- Make negative sentences.
- Form yes/no and WH-questions.
- Recognize common English word order mistakes.

---

## 2. Basic English Sentence Structure

English usually follows this word order:

**Subject + Verb + Object (SVO)**

- Subject: Who or what does the action?
- Verb: What is the action?
- Object: Who or what receives the action?

### Examples

| Subject | Verb | Object |
|---|---|---|
| I | like | coffee. |
| She | reads | books. |
| They | play | soccer. |
| We | watch | movies. |
| He | drives | a car. |

Complete sentences:

- I like coffee.
- She reads books.
- They play soccer.
- We watch movies.
- He drives a car.

**Important:** In normal English statements, the subject usually comes before the verb.

Correct: She likes music.

Incorrect: Likes she music.

---

## 3. Subject + Verb

Some sentences do not need an object.

**Structure:**

Subject + Verb

Examples:

- Birds fly.
- Babies cry.
- The sun shines.
- My father works.
- They sleep.

The verb can describe an action without an object.

---

## 4. Sentences with the Verb "To Be"

The verb "to be" connects the subject to information about the subject.

**Structure:**

Subject + am/is/are + Complement

A complement can be a noun or an adjective.

### 4.1 Subject + Be + Noun

Examples:

- I am a student.
- She is a teacher.
- He is my brother.
- They are friends.
- We are engineers.

### 4.2 Subject + Be + Adjective

Examples:

- I am happy.
- She is tired.
- He is tall.
- They are busy.
- The food is delicious.

**Remember:** Do not use "do" to make a negative sentence or question with the verb "to be."

Correct: Are you happy?

Incorrect: Do you are happy?

Correct: She isn't tired.

Incorrect: She doesn't be tired.

---

## 5. The Position of Adjectives

In English, an adjective usually comes before the noun it describes.

**Structure:**

Adjective + Noun

Examples:

- a beautiful house
- a small dog
- an expensive phone
- a new computer
- a red car
- an interesting book

Complete sentences:

- I have a small dog.
- She bought a new phone.
- They live in a beautiful house.
- He reads an interesting book.

### Adjectives After "To Be"

When an adjective describes the subject after the verb "to be," it comes after the verb.

Examples:

- The house is beautiful.
- My dog is small.
- The phone is expensive.
- The book is interesting.

Compare:

- She has a beautiful bag.
- Her bag is beautiful.

---

## 6. Adverbs of Frequency

Adverbs of frequency describe how often something happens.

Common adverbs:

| Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Always | Every time |
| Usually | Most of the time |
| Often | Many times |
| Sometimes | On some occasions |
| Rarely | Not often |
| Never | At no time |

### 6.1 Before Most Main Verbs

**Structure:**

Subject + Frequency Adverb + Main Verb

Examples:

- I always drink coffee.
- She usually walks to work.
- They often play soccer.
- We sometimes watch TV.
- He never eats fish.

### 6.2 After the Verb "To Be"

**Structure:**

Subject + Be + Frequency Adverb

Examples:

- I am always happy.
- She is usually busy.
- They are often late.
- He is never angry.

**Important:** The position changes depending on the verb.

Correct: She is always friendly.

Correct: She always helps me.

Incorrect: She always is friendly. (Not the usual neutral word order.)

---

## 7. Negative Sentence Structure

English uses different structures to make negative sentences.

### 7.1 With "To Be"

**Structure:**

Subject + am/is/are + not + Complement

Examples:

- I am not tired.
- She is not a doctor.
- He isn't happy.
- They aren't ready.

### 7.2 With Present Simple

Use "do not" or "does not" before the base form of the main verb.

**Structure:**

Subject + do/does + not + Base Verb

Examples:

- I do not like tea.
- She does not play tennis.
- He doesn't work here.
- They don't live in London.

**Important:** After "doesn't," use the base form of the verb.

Correct: She doesn't like coffee.

Incorrect: She doesn't likes coffee.

### 7.3 With Past Simple

Use "did not" before the base form of the verb.

**Structure:**

Subject + did not + Base Verb

Examples:

- I didn't watch TV.
- She didn't call me.
- They didn't go to school.
- He didn't buy a car.

Correct: He didn't go.

Incorrect: He didn't went.

### 7.4 With Modal Verbs

Put "not" after the modal verb.

**Structure:**

Subject + Modal + Not + Base Verb

Examples:

- I cannot swim.
- She shouldn't smoke.
- You mustn't park here.
- They won't come today.

---

## 8. Yes/No Questions

A yes/no question can be answered with "yes" or "no."

### 8.1 Questions with "To Be"

Move the verb "to be" before the subject.

**Structure:**

Am/Is/Are + Subject + Complement?

Examples:

- Are you happy?
- Is she a teacher?
- Are they students?
- Is he at home?

### 8.2 Questions with Present Simple

Use "do" or "does" before the subject.

**Structure:**

Do/Does + Subject + Base Verb?

Examples:

- Do you like coffee?
- Does she work here?
- Do they play soccer?
- Does he speak English?

### 8.3 Questions with Past Simple

Use "did" before the subject.

**Structure:**

Did + Subject + Base Verb?

Examples:

- Did you call me?
- Did she visit her mother?
- Did they watch the movie?
- Did he go to work?

### 8.4 Questions with Modal Verbs

Put the modal verb before the subject.

Examples:

- Can you swim?
- Should I call him?
- Must we leave now?
- Will they come tomorrow?

---

## 9. WH-Question Structure

WH-questions ask for specific information.

Common question words:

| Question Word | Use |
|---|---|
| What | Things or information |
| Where | Places |
| When | Time |
| Who | People |
| Why | Reasons |
| Which | Choices |
| Whose | Possession |
| How | Manner or condition |

### 9.1 Questions with "To Be"

**Structure:**

WH-Word + Am/Is/Are + Subject?

Examples:

- What is your name?
- Where are you from?
- When is your birthday?
- Who is your teacher?
- Why are you tired?

### 9.2 Questions with Present Simple

**Structure:**

WH-Word + Do/Does + Subject + Base Verb?

Examples:

- What do you want?
- Where does she live?
- When do they study?
- Why does he work here?
- How do you go to school?

### 9.3 Questions with Past Simple

**Structure:**

WH-Word + Did + Subject + Base Verb?

Examples:

- What did you buy?
- Where did she go?
- When did they arrive?
- Why did he leave?

### 9.4 Questions with Modal Verbs

Examples:

- What can you do?
- Where should we go?
- When will they arrive?
- How can I help you?

### 9.5 Questions with "Who" as the Subject

When "who" is the subject of a question, we usually do not use "do" or "does."

Examples:

- Who lives here?
- Who speaks English?
- Who works in this office?

Compare:

- Who calls you? (Who performs the action?)
- Who do you call? (Who receives the action?)

---

## 10. Place and Time Expressions

Place and time expressions give additional information.

A common neutral order is:

**Subject + Verb + Object + Place + Time**

Examples:

- I study English at home every day.
- She plays tennis at the park on Saturdays.
- They watch movies at home every weekend.
- We visit our friends in New York every summer.

Place often comes before time when both appear at the end of a sentence.

### More Examples

- He works in an office every morning.
- My parents go to the supermarket on Fridays.
- We eat dinner at a restaurant on weekends.

**Note:** English word order can change when a speaker wants to emphasize a particular detail. The order above is a useful basic pattern, not an absolute rule.

---

## 11. There Is and There Are

Use "there is" and "there are" to say that something exists or is present.

### Singular

**Structure:**

There + Is + Singular Noun

Examples:

- There is a book on the table.
- There is a cat in the garden.
- There is a supermarket near my house.

### Plural

**Structure:**

There + Are + Plural Noun

Examples:

- There are two chairs in the room.
- There are many students in the class.
- There are three windows in the house.

Negative examples:

- There isn't a bank here.
- There aren't any restaurants nearby.

Question examples:

- Is there a bathroom?
- Are there any parks near here?

---

## 12. Imperative Sentences

Use the base form of a verb to give instructions, commands, or directions.

**Structure:**

Base Verb + Rest of Sentence

Examples:

- Open the door.
- Please sit down.
- Read this book.
- Turn left.
- Listen carefully.

For negative instructions, use "don't."

Examples:

- Don't touch that.
- Don't run.
- Don't open the window.
- Don't be late.

The subject "you" is normally understood and is not written.

## 13. Common Word Order Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| Like I coffee. | I like coffee. |
| She a teacher is. | She is a teacher. |
| He likes very music. | He likes music very much. |
| I every day study English. | I study English every day. |
| She doesn't likes tea. | She doesn't like tea. |
| Did you went home? | Did you go home? |
| Where you live? | Where do you live? |
| Are you like pizza? | Do you like pizza? |
| A car red | A red car |
| She always is happy. | She is always happy. |

### Important Rules

1. Put the subject before the verb in normal statements.
2. Put adjectives before nouns.
3. Put frequency adverbs before most main verbs.
4. Put frequency adverbs after the verb "to be."
5. Use "do" or "does" for present simple questions and negatives.
6. Use "did" for past simple questions and negatives.
7. Use the base form of a verb after do, does, and did.
8. Put the verb before the subject in yes/no questions with "to be" and modal verbs.

---

## 14. American English Notes

These sentence patterns are standard in American English.

### Common American Expressions

- I live in New York.
- She works at a hospital.
- We go to school every day.
- Do you want some coffee?
- Where do you work?

### "At the Weekend" vs. "On the Weekend"

American English commonly uses:

- on the weekend
- on weekends

Examples:

- I relax on the weekend.
- We visit our family on weekends.

British English commonly uses "at the weekend."

### Natural Spoken English

In informal American conversation, speakers often use contractions:

- I am → I'm
- She is → She's
- Do not → Don't
- Does not → Doesn't
- Did not → Didn't

Examples:

- I'm happy.
- She doesn't work here.
- They didn't call me.

## 17. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned the basic rules of English sentence structure and word order.

Key points:

- Basic English statements usually follow Subject + Verb + Object.
- Some sentences need only a subject and a verb.
- Use "am," "is," and "are" to connect a subject with a noun or adjective.
- Adjectives usually come before nouns.
- Frequency adverbs usually come before main verbs but after "to be."
- Use "do," "does," and "did" for questions and negatives with ordinary verbs.
- Use the base form of a verb after do, does, and did.
- Put "to be" or a modal verb before the subject in yes/no questions.
- Use WH-words to ask for specific information.
- Place expressions usually come before time expressions when both are at the end of a sentence.

**Final Reminder:** English word order is important. Practice building short, clear sentences before trying to write longer and more complex ones.
