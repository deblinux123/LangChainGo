# Chapter 31 — Adverbs of Time and Place

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand adverbs of time and place.
- Explain when an action happens.
- Explain where an action happens.
- Use common time and place adverbs in sentences.
- Ask questions with **When?** and **Where?**
- Use correct word order in simple sentences.

## 2. What Are Adverbs?

An adverb gives us more information about an action or a sentence.

In this chapter, we will study two types:

1. Adverbs of time: tell us **when** something happens.
2. Adverbs of place: tell us **where** something happens.

Examples:

- I will call you tomorrow. (Time)
- She is waiting outside. (Place)
- He arrived yesterday. (Time)
- The children are playing upstairs. (Place)

## 3. Adverbs of Time

Adverbs of time tell us when an action happens.

They answer the question:

**When?**

Examples:

- I study English every day.
- She arrived yesterday.
- We will travel tomorrow.
- He is working now.
- They are leaving soon.

### Common Adverbs of Time

| Adverb | Example |
|---|---|
| Now | I am busy now. |
| Today | I have a meeting today. |
| Yesterday | She called me yesterday. |
| Tomorrow | We will meet tomorrow. |
| Tonight | I am studying tonight. |
| Soon | The bus will arrive soon. |
| Later | I will call you later. |
| Already | He is already here. |
| Still | She is still sleeping. |
| Early | I wake up early. |
| Late | He arrived late. |

## 4. Using Adverbs of Time in Sentences

Adverbs of time can appear in different positions.

### Pattern 1: At the End of a Sentence

This is a very common position.

Examples:

- I went shopping yesterday.
- She is studying now.
- We will visit our friends tomorrow.
- He arrived late.
- They are coming soon.

### Pattern 2: At the Beginning of a Sentence

We can put an adverb of time at the beginning to emphasize the time.

Examples:

- Yesterday, I visited my grandmother.
- Today, we have an English lesson.
- Tomorrow, she will start her new job.
- Now, let's begin the exercise.

### Pattern 3: Before the Main Verb

Some time adverbs, especially **already**, **still**, and **just**, can appear before the main verb.

Examples:

- I already know the answer.
- She still lives here.
- He just arrived.

With the verb **be**, these adverbs usually come after the verb.

Examples:

- She is already here.
- He is still busy.

## 5. Adverbs of Place

Adverbs of place tell us where an action happens.

They answer the question:

**Where?**

Examples:

- Come here.
- Please wait outside.
- The children are playing upstairs.
- She looked everywhere.
- He went downstairs.

### Common Adverbs of Place

| Adverb | Example |
|---|---|
| Here | Come here. |
| There | Put your bag there. |
| Inside | Please come inside. |
| Outside | The children are playing outside. |
| Upstairs | My bedroom is upstairs. |
| Downstairs | My parents are downstairs. |
| Everywhere | I looked everywhere. |
| Somewhere | I left my keys somewhere. |
| Away | Please stay away. |
| Nearby | There is a supermarket nearby. |
| Abroad | She lives abroad. |

## 6. Using Adverbs of Place in Sentences

Adverbs of place usually come after the verb or after the object.

### Pattern 1: After the Verb

**Subject + Verb + Adverb of Place**

Examples:

- The children are playing outside.
- Please come inside.
- He lives nearby.
- She went upstairs.
- The dog is sleeping downstairs.

### Pattern 2: After the Object

**Subject + Verb + Object + Adverb of Place**

Examples:

- Put your shoes here.
- Leave the bags outside.
- Take the children upstairs.
- I found my phone there.

## 7. Asking Questions with When and Where

Use **When** to ask about time.

Use **Where** to ask about place.

### Questions with When

Examples:

- When do you study English?
- I study English every day.

- When did she arrive?
- She arrived yesterday.

- When will they come?
- They will come tomorrow.

### Questions with Where

Examples:

- Where do you live?
- I live in Chicago.

- Where are the children playing?
- They are playing outside.

- Where is your brother?
- He is upstairs.

## 8. Adverbs of Time vs. Adverbs of Place

| Adverb Type | Question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Time | When? | Today, yesterday, tomorrow |
| Place | Where? | Here, there, outside |
| Time | When? | Now, soon, later |
| Place | Where? | Upstairs, downstairs, nearby |

Compare:

- She is studying **now**. (Time)
- She is studying **upstairs**. (Place)

- They arrived **yesterday**. (Time)
- They are waiting **outside**. (Place)

## 9. Using Time and Place Adverbs Together

When a sentence includes both an adverb of place and an adverb of time, the place adverb usually comes before the time adverb.

**Common Pattern:**

Subject + Verb + Place + Time

Examples:

- I will meet you there tomorrow.
- She is working upstairs today.
- They played outside yesterday.
- We are staying here tonight.

This is a common pattern, not an absolute rule. Other word orders may be possible depending on emphasis.

## 10. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Using the wrong question word

Incorrect: Where will you arrive?

Correct: When will you arrive? (Asking about time.)

Correct: Where will you arrive? (Asking about place.)

### Mistake 2: Incorrect word order

Incorrect: She tomorrow will come.

Correct: She will come tomorrow.

### Mistake 3: Incorrect placement of place adverbs

Incorrect: They outside are playing.

Correct: They are playing outside.

### Mistake 4: Confusing time and place

- Tomorrow tells us when.
- Upstairs tells us where.
- Yesterday tells us when.
- Outside tells us where.

### Mistake 5: Confusing "there" and "their"

- There is an adverb of place: Put the book there.
- Their is a possessive determiner: Their house is big.

## 11. American English Notes

These adverbs are common in everyday American English.

Examples:

- "I'll see you tomorrow."
- "Come inside!"
- "Wait outside."
- "I'll call you later."
- "She's upstairs."
- "Let's go somewhere nearby."

In casual American English, **right now** is often used to emphasize that something is happening immediately.

Examples:

- I need your help right now.
- Please come here right now.

## 14. Chapter Summary

In this chapter, you learned:

- Adverbs of time tell us when something happens.
- Adverbs of place tell us where something happens.
- Adverbs of time often appear at the end or beginning of a sentence.
- Adverbs of place usually appear after the verb or object.
- Use **When** to ask about time.
- Use **Where** to ask about place.
- Place adverbs usually come before time adverbs when both appear at the end of a sentence.
