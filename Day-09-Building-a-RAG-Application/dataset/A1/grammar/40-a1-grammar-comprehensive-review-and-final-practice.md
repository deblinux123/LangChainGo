# Part 1: Grammar Review

## 2. Subject Pronouns and the Verb "To Be"

Subject pronouns replace people's or things' names.

| Subject Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|
| I | I am a student. |
| You | You are friendly. |
| He | He is my brother. |
| She | She is a teacher. |
| It | It is a book. |
| We | We are friends. |
| They | They are students. |

### Forms of "To Be"

| Subject | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | am not |
| You | are | aren't |
| He | is | isn't |
| She | is | isn't |
| It | is | isn't |
| We | are | aren't |
| They | are | aren't |

Examples:

- I am happy.
- She is not tired.
- They are students.
- We aren't at home.

### Questions

Put "am," "is," or "are" before the subject.

- Are you a student?
- Is she your sister?
- Are they at home?

Short answers:

- Yes, I am.
- No, she isn't.
- Yes, they are.

## 3. Articles: A, An, and The

Use "a" before a singular countable noun that begins with a consonant sound.

- a book
- a car
- a teacher
- a university

Use "an" before a singular countable noun that begins with a vowel sound.

- an apple
- an egg
- an orange
- an hour

Use "the" when the listener knows which person or thing you mean, or when the noun is specific.

Examples:

- I have a dog.
- The dog is very friendly.
- She bought an umbrella.
- Please close the door.

**Remember:** Choose "a" or "an" based on the first sound, not simply the first letter.

## 4. Nouns: Singular, Plural, Countable, and Uncountable

### Singular and Plural

Most nouns form the plural with "-s."

- book → books
- car → cars
- student → students

Some nouns use "-es."

- box → boxes
- watch → watches
- bus → buses

Some nouns have irregular plural forms.

- man → men
- woman → women
- child → children
- person → people

### Countable Nouns

Countable nouns can be counted.

- one apple
- two apples
- three books

### Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable nouns are not normally counted individually.

- water
- milk
- rice
- information
- furniture

Examples:

- I need some water.
- She has two books.
- There is some milk in the refrigerator.

## 6. Demonstratives: This, That, These, and Those

| Word | Use |
|---|---|
| This | One thing nearby |
| That | One thing farther away |
| These | Multiple things nearby |
| Those | Multiple things farther away |

Examples:

- This is my phone.
- That is your car.
- These are my books.
- Those are beautiful flowers.

Remember:

- This book is new.
- These books are new.

## 7. There Is and There Are

Use "there is" with singular nouns and uncountable nouns.

Examples:

- There is a chair in the room.
- There is some water in the bottle.

Use "there are" with plural nouns.

Examples:

- There are two chairs in the room.
- There are many students here.

Negative:

- There isn't a bank nearby.
- There aren't any apples.

Questions:

- Is there a supermarket?
- Are there any restaurants?

## 9. Present Simple

Use the present simple for:

- Daily routines.
- Habits.
- General facts.
- Regular activities.

### Positive Sentences

**Structure:**

Subject + Base Verb

For he, she, and it, add "-s" or "-es" to most verbs.

Examples:

- I work every day.
- You speak English.
- She works in a hospital.
- He watches TV at night.
- They live in Chicago.

### Negative Sentences

**Structure:**

Subject + do/does + not + Base Verb

Examples:

- I don't like coffee.
- She doesn't work here.
- They don't play tennis.
- He doesn't speak French.

### Questions

**Structure:**

Do/Does + Subject + Base Verb?

Examples:

- Do you speak English?
- Does she work here?
- Do they live in New York?

### Frequency Adverbs

- always
- usually
- often
- sometimes
- rarely
- never

Examples:

- I always drink water.
- She usually walks to work.
- He is never late.

## 10. Present Continuous

Use the present continuous for actions happening now or around the present time.

**Structure:**

Subject + am/is/are + Verb-ing

Examples:

- I am studying English.
- She is cooking dinner.
- They are playing soccer.
- We are watching a movie.

### Negative

- I am not sleeping.
- He isn't working.
- They aren't studying.

### Questions

- Are you listening?
- Is she cooking?
- Are they coming?

### Common Spelling Changes

| Base Verb | -ing Form |
|---|---|
| work | working |
| read | reading |
| make | making |
| write | writing |
| run | running |
| swim | swimming |

---

## 11. Present Simple vs. Present Continuous

| Present Simple | Present Continuous |
|---|---|
| Habits and routines | Actions happening now |
| I work every day. | I am working now. |
| She reads books. | She is reading a book. |
| They play soccer on Sundays. | They are playing soccer now. |

Compare:

- He usually drives to work.
- He is taking the bus today.

## 12. Past Simple

Use the past simple for completed actions in the past.

### Regular Verbs

Add "-ed" to most regular verbs.

| Base Verb | Past Form |
|---|---|
| work | worked |
| play | played |
| watch | watched |
| visit | visited |
| clean | cleaned |

Examples:

- I worked yesterday.
- She visited her friend.
- They watched a movie.

### Irregular Verbs

| Base Verb | Past Form |
|---|---|
| go | went |
| see | saw |
| eat | ate |
| have | had |
| buy | bought |
| come | came |
| make | made |
| take | took |
| write | wrote |
| get | got |

Examples:

- I went to school.
- She bought a new phone.
- They ate dinner.

### Past of "To Be"

| Subject | Past Form |
|---|---|
| I | was |
| He/She/It | was |
| You/We/They | were |

Examples:

- I was tired.
- She was at home.
- They were happy.

### Negative Sentences

Use "didn't" with the base form of the verb.

- I didn't work yesterday.
- She didn't go to school.
- They didn't watch TV.

### Questions

Use "did" before the subject.

- Did you call me?
- Did she buy a car?
- Did they visit you?

**Important:** After "did" and "didn't," use the base form of the verb.

Correct: Did you go?

Incorrect: Did you went?

## 13. Future: Going To and Will

### Going To

Use "going to" for plans and intentions.

**Structure:**

Subject + am/is/are + going to + Base Verb

Examples:

- I am going to study tonight.
- She is going to visit her parents.
- They are going to travel next week.

### Will

Use "will" for predictions, decisions made at the moment, and promises.

**Structure:**

Subject + will + Base Verb

Examples:

- I will call you later.
- It will rain tomorrow.
- She will help us.

Negative:

- I won't forget.
- He won't come today.

Questions:

- Will you help me?
- Will they arrive soon?

---

## 14. Can, Should, and Must

### Can and Can't

Use "can" for ability and permission.

- I can swim.
- She can drive.
- He can't speak Japanese.
- Can you help me?

### Should and Shouldn't

Use "should" to give advice.

- You should study more.
- She should see a doctor.
- You shouldn't eat too much sugar.

### Must and Mustn't

Use "must" for strong obligation and "mustn't" for prohibition.

- You must wear a seat belt.
- Students must follow the rules.
- You mustn't smoke here.

**Remember:** Use the base form of the verb after modal verbs.

Correct: She can swim.

Incorrect: She can swims.

## 15. Adjectives and Comparisons

Adjectives describe nouns.

Examples:

- a beautiful house
- a small dog
- an expensive phone
- a friendly teacher

### Comparative Adjectives

Use comparative adjectives to compare two people or things.

| Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| small | smaller |
| tall | taller |
| fast | faster |
| big | bigger |
| happy | happier |
| beautiful | more beautiful |
| expensive | more expensive |

Examples:

- My house is bigger than your house.
- Anna is taller than John.
- This phone is more expensive than that one.

### Superlative Adjectives

Use superlatives to describe the highest or lowest degree within a group.

| Adjective | Superlative |
|---|---|
| small | the smallest |
| tall | the tallest |
| big | the biggest |
| happy | the happiest |
| beautiful | the most beautiful |
| expensive | the most expensive |

Examples:

- He is the tallest student in the class.
- This is the most expensive phone.
- She is the happiest person here.

## 16. Adverbs

Adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.

### Adverbs of Manner

Examples:

- She speaks slowly.

- They work hard.
- The baby sleeps peacefully.

### Adverbs of Time

Examples:

- I will call you tomorrow.
- She arrived yesterday.
- We are leaving now.
- He works today.

### Adverbs of Place

Examples:

- Come here.
- Please sit there.

## 17. Gerunds and Infinitives

### Gerunds

A gerund is the "-ing" form of a verb used as a noun.

Examples:

- Swimming is fun.
- I enjoy reading.
- She likes cooking.
- He loves playing soccer.

### Infinitives

An infinitive is "to" plus the base form of a verb.

Examples:

- I want to learn English.
- She needs to study.
- They plan to travel.
- He decided to leave.

Some verbs are commonly followed by gerunds, while others are followed by infinitives.

| Verb | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| enjoy | Verb-ing | I enjoy reading. |
| finish | Verb-ing | She finished working. |
| want | to + Verb | I want to sleep. |
| need | to + Verb | He needs to study. |
| decide | to + Verb | They decided to leave. |

---

## 18. Relative Clauses: Who, Which, and That

Use "who" for people.

- The woman who lives here is a doctor.
- I know a boy who speaks English.

Use "which" for things and animals.

- This is the book which I bought.
- I have a dog which likes water.

Use "that" for people or things in many defining relative clauses.

- The man that works here is friendly.
- This is the phone that I want.

## 19. Conjunctions and Sentence Structure

| Connector | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| And | Addition | I like tea and coffee. |
| But | Contrast | I am tired, but I am happy. |
| Or | Choice | Do you want tea or juice? |
| Because | Reason | I am home because I am sick. |
| So | Result | It is raining, so I am staying home. |
| When | Time | I smile when I see you. |

Basic statement order:

**Subject + Verb + Object**

Examples:

- I like music.
- She reads books.
- They play soccer.

Questions and negatives need the correct auxiliary or modal verb.

## 20. Question Words and Question Tags

### WH-Questions

| Word | Use |
|---|---|
| What | Things and information |
| Where | Places |
| When | Time |
| Who | People |
| Why | Reasons |
| Which | Choices |
| Whose | Possession |
| How | Manner or condition |

Examples:

- What is your name?
- Where do you live?
- When does the class start?
- Who is your teacher?
- Why are you sad?
- How do you go to work?

### Basic Question Tags

Question tags are short questions added to statements.

Examples:

- You're a student, aren't you?
- She is happy, isn't she?
- They aren't busy, are they?

At A1 level, focus on recognizing and using simple forms.

| Full Form | Contraction |

| Will not | Won't |
| Is not | Isn't |
| Are not | Aren't |

Examples:

- I'm a student.
- She doesn't like coffee.
