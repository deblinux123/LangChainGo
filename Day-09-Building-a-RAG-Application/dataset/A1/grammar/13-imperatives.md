# Chapter 13: Imperatives — Commands and Instructions

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Give simple commands and instructions.
- Use the base form of a verb to make imperatives.
- Make negative commands with don't.
- Use please to make instructions more polite.
- Make simple suggestions using let's.

## 2. What Are Imperatives?

We use **imperatives** to give commands, instructions, directions, warnings, and advice.

Examples:

- Open the door.
- Sit down.
- Listen carefully.
- Turn left.
- Be careful!

The subject **you** is usually understood, so we do not normally write it.

Compare:

- You open the door. (A statement or instruction with an explicit subject.)
- Open the door. (An imperative.)

## 3. Positive Imperatives

To make a positive imperative, use the base form of the verb.

Structure:

**Base verb + ...**

Examples:

- Close the window.
- Read this book.
- Write your name.
- Turn on the computer.
- Please wait here.

The verb does not change for different people.

Examples:

- Sit down.
- Stand up.
- Listen to me.
- Follow the instructions.

## 4. Negative Imperatives

Use **don't + base verb** to tell someone not to do something.

Structure:

**Don't + base verb + ...**

Examples:

- Don't touch that.
- Don't open the window.
- Don't run.
- Don't be late.
- Don't forget your keys.

The full form is **do not**, but don't is very common in everyday American English.

Examples:

- Do not enter.
- Don't park here.
- Don't use your phone during the exam.

## 5. Using Please

We use **please** to make commands and instructions more polite.

Please can appear at the beginning or the end of a sentence.

Examples:

- Please sit down.
- Please open the door.
- Wait here, please.
- Close the window, please.
- Please don't make noise.

Compare:

- Give me your phone. (Direct command.)
- Please give me your phone. (More polite.)

## 6. Common Everyday Imperatives

### At Home

- Clean your room.
- Wash the dishes.
- Turn off the TV.
- Open the window.
- Take out the trash.

### In the Classroom

- Open your books.
- Read the text.
- Listen carefully.
- Write your answers.
- Work in pairs.

### In Public Places

- Keep quiet.
- Wait in line.
- Don't smoke here.
- Don't park here.
- Keep the door closed.

## 7. Giving Directions

Imperatives are commonly used when giving directions.

Examples:

- Go straight.
- Turn left.
- Turn right.
- Cross the street.
- Stop at the traffic light.
- Walk to the next corner.
- Take the first street on your right.

## 8. Let's for Suggestions

Use **let's + base verb** to make suggestions that include yourself and another person or group.

Structure:

**Let's + base verb + ...**

Examples:

- Let's go.
- Let's eat lunch.
- Let's study English.
- Let's watch a movie.
- Let's play soccer.

The negative form is:

**Let's not + base verb + ...**

Examples:

- Let's not argue.
- Let's not be late.
- Let's not forget the tickets.

## 9. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| To open the door. | Open the door. |
| Opens your book. | Open your book. |
| Don't to run. | Don't run. |
| Doesn't touch that. | Don't touch that. |
| Let's to go home. | Let's go home. |
| Please to sit down. | Please sit down. |

Remember:

- Use the base form of the verb.
- Do not use to before the verb.
- Use don't for negative imperatives.
- Use let's for suggestions that include the speaker.

## 10. American English Notes

- Don't is very common in everyday American speech.
- Please is often used to make requests and instructions more polite.
- "Take out the trash" is a common American expression for removing garbage from the home.
- "Line up" and "Wait in line" are common expressions for forming or waiting in a queue.

## 13. Chapter Summary

- Imperatives give commands, instructions, directions, and warnings.
- Use the base form of a verb for positive imperatives.
- Use don't + base verb for negative imperatives.
- Use please to make commands more polite.
- Use let's + base verb to make suggestions that include the speaker.
- Use let's not + base verb for negative suggestions.
