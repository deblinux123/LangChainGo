# Chapter 14: Present Continuous

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Describe actions happening now.
- Form positive, negative, and question sentences.
- Use am, is, and are with the -ing form of verbs.
- Understand common spelling rules for -ing.
- Distinguish between Present Simple and Present Continuous.

## 2. What Is the Present Continuous?

We use the **Present Continuous** to talk about actions happening now or around the present time.

Examples:

- I am studying English.
- She is cooking dinner.
- They are playing soccer.
- He is watching TV.
- We are learning grammar.

Common time expressions:

- now
- right now
- at the moment
- today
- this week

Examples:

- I am working right now.
- She is studying at the moment.
- We are learning English this week.

## 3. Positive Sentences

Structure:

**Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing + ...**

Choose the correct form of the verb to be:

| Subject | Auxiliary Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | I am working. |
| You | are | You are reading. |
| He | is | He is sleeping. |
| She | is | She is cooking. |
| It | is | It is raining. |
| We | are | We are studying. |
| They | are | They are playing. |

Examples:

- I am writing an email.
- You are listening to music.
- My brother is playing a video game.
- The children are running in the park.

## 4. Contractions

In everyday English, contractions are very common.

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| I am | I'm |
| You are | You're |
| He is | He's |
| She is | She's |
| It is | It's |
| We are | We're |
| They are | They're |

Examples:

- I'm studying.
- She's working.
- We're watching a movie.
- They're having lunch.

## 5. Negative Sentences

To make a negative sentence, add **not** after am, is, or are.

Structure:

**Subject + am/is/are + not + verb-ing + ...**

Examples:

- I am not sleeping.
- She is not working.
- They are not studying.
- He is not watching TV.

### Negative Contractions

| Full Form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| is not | isn't |
| are not | aren't |

Examples:

- She isn't cooking.
- They aren't playing.
- He isn't listening.

With I, we commonly say:

- I'm not working.

## 6. Questions

To make a question, put am, is, or are before the subject.

Structure:

**Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing + ...?**

Examples:

- Are you studying?
- Is she cooking?
- Is he sleeping?
- Are they playing soccer?
- Am I speaking too fast?

### Short Answers

| Question | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Are you working? | Yes, I am. | No, I'm not. |
| Is she studying? | Yes, she is. | No, she isn't. |
| Is he sleeping? | Yes, he is. | No, he isn't. |
| Are they playing? | Yes, they are. | No, they aren't. |

## 7. Wh- Questions

We can use question words to ask for more information.

Structure:

**Question word + am/is/are + subject + verb-ing?**

Examples:

- What are you doing?
- Where is she going?
- Why are they laughing?
- Who is he talking to?
- What are you watching?

Possible answers:

- I'm studying.
- She's going to school.
- They're laughing because the movie is funny.
- He's talking to his friend.
- We're watching a movie.

## 8. Spelling Rules for -ing

Most verbs add **-ing** directly.

| Base Verb | -ing Form |
|---|---|
| work | working |
| read | reading |
| play | playing |
| watch | watching |
| eat | eating |

### Rule 1: Drop the Final -e

For many verbs ending in silent -e, remove the -e and add -ing.

| Base Verb | -ing Form |
|---|---|
| make | making |
| write | writing |
| dance | dancing |
| come | coming |
| take | taking |

### Rule 2: Double the Final Consonant

For many short verbs ending in consonant-vowel-consonant, double the final consonant before adding -ing.

| Base Verb | -ing Form |
|---|---|
| run | running |
| sit | sitting |
| swim | swimming |
| stop | stopping |
| get | getting |

This rule depends on the word's spelling and stress pattern; not every verb ending in a consonant follows it.

### Rule 3: Verbs Ending in -ie

Change -ie to -y and add -ing.

| Base Verb | -ing Form |
|---|---|
| lie | lying |
| die | dying |
| tie | tying |

## 9. Present Simple vs. Present Continuous

We use Present Simple for habits, routines, and general facts.

We use Present Continuous for actions happening now or around the present time.

| Present Simple | Present Continuous |
|---|---|
| I work every day. | I am working now. |
| She reads every night. | She is reading now. |
| They play soccer on Sundays. | They are playing soccer now. |
| He drinks coffee every morning. | He is drinking coffee right now. |

Compare:

- I study English every day. (A regular habit.)
- I am studying English now. (An action happening now.)

## 10. Stative Verbs

Some verbs usually describe states rather than actions. They are not commonly used in the continuous form at beginner level.

Common examples:

- know
- like
- love
- want
- need
- understand
- believe

Examples:

- I know the answer.
- She likes coffee.
- We need some water.
- He understands the question.

Avoid these forms in their usual meanings:

- I am knowing the answer.
- She is liking coffee.

Some verbs can have both simple and continuous forms with different meanings or in different contexts. We will study those distinctions later.

## 11. Common Mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| I am study English. | I am studying English. |
| She are cooking. | She is cooking. |
| They is playing. | They are playing. |
| He not working. | He is not working. |
| Are she sleeping? | Is she sleeping? |
| I am knowing him. | I know him. |
| He is runing. | He is running. |

## 12. American English Notes

- Present Continuous is used frequently in everyday American conversation to describe current activities.
- "What are you doing?" is a very common everyday question.
- "I'm getting ready" is a common expression meaning that someone is preparing to leave or do something.
- The spelling rules for the -ing form are generally the same in American and British English.

## 15. Chapter Summary

- Use Present Continuous for actions happening now or around the present time.
- Structure: subject + am/is/are + verb-ing.
- Add not after am, is, or are to make negative sentences.
- Put am, is, or are before the subject to make questions.
- Use the -ing spelling rules correctly.
- Use Present Simple for habits and routines.
- Some stative verbs, such as know, like, and want, are not commonly used in continuous forms in their usual meanings.
